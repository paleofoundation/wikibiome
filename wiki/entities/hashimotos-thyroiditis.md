# Hashimoto's Thyroiditis

## Overview

Hashimoto's thyroiditis (HT) is the most common autoimmune disease globally and the leading cause of hypothyroidism. Chronic lymphocytic infiltration of the thyroid gland progressively destroys follicular architecture, reducing thyroid hormone output. Prevalence is 5-10% with a striking 10:1 female-to-male ratio. Diagnosis rests on elevated anti-TPO and anti-Tg antibodies, often with rising TSH and declining free T4. Concordance in monozygotic twins (~35%) indicates shared genetic susceptibility (HLA-DRB1, PTPN22) with environmental triggers. Unlike [[graves-disease]], which causes hyperthyroidism via stimulatory TRAb, HT produces destructive autoimmunity -- yet both are autoimmune thyroid diseases (AITD) with overlapping genetic architecture and mineral dependencies. <!-- UNSOURCED: needs attribution -->

**Prevalence and antibodies (2026 evidence update).** A meta-analysis of 48 observational studies (about 22.7 million participants in 19 countries) estimated a global adult HT prevalence of 7.5% (95% CI 5.7 to 9.6%), with women about four times as likely to be affected as men (OR 3.86, 95% CI 2.33 to 6.39) [[hu-2022-global-prevalence-hashimotos-thyroiditis-meta-analysis]]. Pooled prevalence was 9.0% in studies published before 2000 and 6.5% after, and ranged from 5.8% in Asia to 14.2% in Africa, but heterogeneity was extreme (I2 = 100%) and 27 of the 48 studies defined HT by antibodies alone, so these figures partly reflect antibody positivity [[hu-2022-global-prevalence-hashimotos-thyroiditis-meta-analysis]]. This pooled estimate implies a much smaller female excess than the 10:1 ratio quoted above [[hu-2022-global-prevalence-hashimotos-thyroiditis-meta-analysis]].

Anti-TPO antibodies are reported in about 90 to 95% of people with autoimmune thyroid disease and anti-Tg antibodies in 60 to 80% of people with HT (narrative review) [[frohlich-2017-anti-thyroid-antibodies-thyroid-extra-thyroidal-diseases]]. Anti-TPO can fix complement, but the review attributes most thyroid cell destruction to T-cell and cytokine-mediated apoptosis rather than to the antibodies themselves, and notes that anti-TPO titers fall only slowly on levothyroxine [[frohlich-2017-anti-thyroid-antibodies-thyroid-extra-thyroidal-diseases]]. In one population cited by the same review, salt iodization raised anti-TPO prevalence from 14.3% to 23.8% [[frohlich-2017-anti-thyroid-antibodies-thyroid-extra-thyroidal-diseases]].

## Metallomic Signature

### Selenium -- The Critical Protector

The thyroid has the highest [[selenium]] concentration of any organ, owing to selenoprotein-dependent hormone synthesis and antioxidant defense (GPx, TrxR, DIO1-3). Se supplementation at 200 ug/day reduces anti-TPO antibodies by up to 40% in patients with levels >1200 IU/mL. Se modulates Th1/Th2/Th17/Treg balance, increases regulatory T cells, and protects against [[oxidative-stress]] generated by H2O2 during thyroid hormone synthesis. Se also antagonizes [[cadmium]] and [[mercury]] toxicity through direct binding and biliary excretion.

### Iron Deficiency

58% of HT patients have [[iron]] deficiency anemia. Fe is essential for TPO (thyroid peroxidase) activity; deficiency impairs T4 synthesis and elevates TSH. Meta-analysis of 47 studies (53,152 pregnant women) shows Fe deficiency associates with higher TSH (2.31 vs 1.75 mIU/L) and lower free T4. Fe also modulates M1/M2 macrophage polarization relevant to autoimmune regulation, connecting to [[nutritional-immunity]] dynamics.

### Zinc and Copper

[[zinc]] is a cofactor for >300 metalloenzymes including those in the TRH-TSH pathway and deiodinases. Low Zn prevalence reaches 49.1% in hypothyroid patients (OR 5.926). Zn mediates IL-1, IL-6, and TNF-alpha synthesis and is required for thymulin activation. [[copper]] serves as cofactor for superoxide dismutase (SOD) and stimulates T4 production; the Cu/Zn ratio is characteristically altered in thyroid autoimmunity.

### Iodine Excess Paradox

[[iodine]] has a U-shaped dose-response with AITD. Excess iodine inhibits TPO activity via the Wolff-Chaikoff effect, activates the NLRP3 inflammasome, promotes Th17 proliferation, and generates ROS in thyroid cells. Autoimmune thyroiditis prevalence increases after salt iodization programs. In combined Se and I deficiency, normalizing Se without iodine worsens hypothyroidism -- the elements must be balanced together.

### Heavy Metals and Nickel

Cd inhibits hepatic 5'-monodeiodinase (T4-to-T3 conversion); Pb prevents deiodination; Hg inhibits TPO and Tg iodination. Nickel shows a dose-response relationship with thyroid function parameters: at blood Ni levels of 1.36-60.9 ug/L, 78.7% of men may face 10% higher risk of thyroid dysfunction, operating through [[oxidative-stress]] and apoptotic disruption in thyroid tissue. See [[gut-metal-microbiome]] and [[metal-disease-matrix]].

## Gut-Thyroid Axis

HT patients show significantly reduced alpha diversity (Shannon, Chao1; p<0.001) and distinct microbiome composition vs controls. Iodine-driven [[dysbiosis]] reduces [[butyrate]] and disrupts Th17/Treg balance -- sodium butyrate supplementation partially rescues this phenotype in mouse models. The relationship is bidirectional: dysbiosis impairs mineral absorption (I, Se, Fe) while thyroid hormones (FT3, FT4) regulate specific taxa. Molecular mimicry between gut bacterial antigens and thyroid proteins provides a mechanistic bridge. Gender intensifies microbial differences as HT progresses, with Bifidobacterium much more abundant in females. <!-- UNSOURCED: needs attribution -->

**Intestinal barrier markers.** Three case-control studies report higher circulating [[zonulin]], a marker used as a proxy for [[intestinal-permeability]], in HT. In 77 newly diagnosed Turkish adults, plasma zonulin was higher than in 66 matched controls (7.7 vs 6.4 ng/mL, p<=0.001) and stayed independently associated with HT after adjustment (OR 1.55, 95% CI 1.21 to 2.00), though it discriminated only modestly (AUC 0.705) [[demir-2022-plasma-zonulin-hashimotos-thyroiditis]]. In 40 Brazilian patients, serum zonulin was also higher than in 53 controls (p=0.002) [[cayres-2021-gut-microbiota-intestinal-permeability-hashimotos]]. A pilot study of 30 children with HT found higher zonulin than in 30 children with congenital hypothyroidism (59.1 vs 43.3 ng/mL, p=0.035), but zonulin tracked BMI and levothyroxine dose rather than anti-TPO or anti-Tg [[kucukemre-aydin-2020-zonulin-intestinal-permeability-pediatric-hashimotos]]. None of these studies measured permeability directly, and the ELISA kits differ enough that absolute values cannot be compared across studies [[demir-2022-plasma-zonulin-hashimotos-thyroiditis]].

**Th17/Treg imbalance.** HT is associated with a shift in the [[th17-treg-balance]] toward inflammatory Th17 cells. In 53 untreated, newly diagnosed patients, circulating Th17 cells were higher (6.38 vs 3.12% of CD4+ T cells) and regulatory T cells lower (3.82 vs 5.61%) than in 21 controls (both p<0.001), with altered PD-1 expression on both subsets [[fang-2022-th17-treg-pd1-pdl1-hashimotos]]. A smaller study of 25 patients found fewer Tregs, lower Foxp3 mRNA, and a higher Th17/Treg ratio that correlated with anti-Tg (r=0.52), along with raised IL-6, IL-23 and GITRL; the rise in Th17 cells alone did not reach significance (p=0.056) [[liu-2014-th17-treg-imbalance-gitrl-hashimotos]]. Mendelian randomization of 165 Treg traits suggests that genetically higher Treg levels are modestly protective (European ORs 0.926 to 0.983, with larger effects in a small East Asian cohort), but no single trait replicated in both ancestries (genetic inference, not a clinical finding) [[guo-2024-treg-cells-hashimotos-mendelian-randomization]].

**Linking microbes to T cells.** A 2024 narrative review proposes that loss of microbial short-chain fatty acids tilts the Th17/Treg balance toward Th17 and so promotes thyroid autoimmunity [[yan-2024-gut-microbiota-metabolites-aitd-review]]. The direct evidence it cites for SCFA-driven Treg expansion comes from colitis, lupus, arthritis and encephalomyelitis mouse models and from TSHR-immunized Graves' mice, and fecal transfer from Graves' patients worsened disease in mice (non-clinical, animal model); the review cites no experiment in a Hashimoto's animal model [[yan-2024-gut-microbiota-metabolites-aitd-review]]. For HT, the microbe-to-Treg link therefore remains a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated mechanism [[yan-2024-gut-microbiota-metabolites-aitd-review]].

## Microbiome Signature

Enriched in HT: Proteobacteria, Actinobacteria, [[blautia|Blautia]], Dorea, Lachnospira, Bifidobacterium, Akkermansia. Depleted: Faecalibacterium, Roseburia, [[short-chain-fatty-acids|SCFA]]-producing Firmicutes. Mendelian randomization provides causal evidence: [[akkermansia-muciniphila|Akkermansia]] is strongly protective (OR=0.71, p=9.9E-14) mediated through effector memory CD4+ T cells and IL-6/TNF-alpha modulation. [[prevotella|Prevotella]] associations are context-dependent. Intestinimonas and Turicibacter increase HT risk. Altered bile acid and [[tryptophan-metabolism]] characterize the HT metabolome. <!-- UNSOURCED: needs attribution -->

**What the newer pooled evidence shows (2026 update).** Pooled data do not support a uniform loss of diversity in HT, which conflicts with the reduced-diversity statement in the Gut-Thyroid Axis section above. A meta-analysis of 8 studies found higher Chao1 richness in HT than in controls (SMD 0.68, 95% CI 0.16 to 1.20) but lower richness in [[graves-disease]] (SMD -0.87) [[gong-2021-gut-microbiota-autoimmune-thyroid-disease-meta-analysis]]. A systematic review of 16 studies likewise found higher ACE and Chao1 richness in HT, although this rested on only two HT studies [[sawicka-gutaj-2022-microbiota-autoimmune-thyroid-diseases-systematic-review]]. Single studies diverge: in 63 Han Chinese women with HT, richness and Shannon diversity were lower than in 34 controls [[liu-2020-gut-microbiota-hashimotos-euthyroid-hypothyroid]], whereas in 67 newly diagnosed patients from Hangzhou richness was higher in euthyroid HT but lower in hypothyroid and hyperthyroid HT [[li-2026-thyroid-functional-state-gut-dysbiosis-hashimotos]]. Thyroid functional state, treatment and sex mix may explain much of this disagreement [[li-2026-thyroid-functional-state-gut-dysbiosis-hashimotos]].

**Genus-level findings.** Across autoimmune thyroid disease, pooled [[bifidobacterium]] (SMD -1.08) and [[lactobacillus]] (SMD -0.86) were lower than in controls; the meta-analysis did not report these for HT alone [[gong-2021-gut-microbiota-autoimmune-thyroid-disease-meta-analysis]]. Targeted qPCR in 40 Brazilian HT patients found Bifidobacterium lower (median 70.9 vs 830.6 relative units, p=0.005) and [[bacteroides]] about tenfold higher (2,344 vs 221.3, p<0.0001) [[cayres-2021-gut-microbiota-intestinal-permeability-hashimotos]]. Direction is not consistent: Bifidobacterium was higher, and Lactobacillus and [[blautia|Blautia]] lower, in hypothyroid and hyperthyroid HT in the Hangzhou cohort [[li-2026-thyroid-functional-state-gut-dysbiosis-hashimotos]], and in pooled HT data neither Bacteroidetes (39.0 vs 33.9%, p=0.628) nor Bifidobacterium (p=0.794) differed from controls [[sawicka-gutaj-2022-microbiota-autoimmune-thyroid-diseases-systematic-review]]. [[faecalibacterium|Faecalibacterium]] marked healthy controls, while hypothyroid HT was enriched in Phascolarctobacterium, Bacteroidetes and [[prevotella|Prevotella]] [[liu-2020-gut-microbiota-hashimotos-euthyroid-hypothyroid]]. In hypothyroid HT, Clostridium sensu stricto 1 correlated with TPO antibodies (r=0.21) and inversely with FT3 (r=-0.29) [[li-2026-thyroid-functional-state-gut-dysbiosis-hashimotos]].

**Genetic (Mendelian randomization) signals.** These are genetic inferences, not clinical findings. Using FinnGen (244 autoimmune thyroiditis cases), genetically predicted Alcaligenaceae (OR 0.25), Pasteurellaceae, Peptococcaceae, [[lachnospira|Lachnospira]] and Victivallis were associated with lower risk, with no correction for testing about 210 taxa [[xiong-2024-gut-microbiota-autoimmune-thyroiditis-mendelian-randomization]]. A larger [[mendelian-randomization]] analysis (15,654 HT cases) found nominal protective signals for [[akkermansia-muciniphila|Akkermansia]], Butyrivibrio, Prevotella7 and Ruminococcaceae UCG011 and nominal risk signals for Anaerostipes and Turicibacter, none of which survived FDR correction [[zhang-2024-gut-microbiota-metabolites-aitd-mendelian-randomization]]. That analysis draws on the same HT genome-wide dataset as the Akkermansia result cited above, so it is not an independent replication [[zhang-2024-gut-microbiota-metabolites-aitd-mendelian-randomization]].

**Oral microbiome.** In 60 euthyroid women with HT and 18 controls, salivary alpha and beta diversity did not differ, but Gemella, Enterococcus, Staphylococcus and Bacillus were enriched in HT (p<0.001 each, without false-discovery correction), and levothyroxine use made no difference [[erdem-2023-oral-microbiota-euthyroid-hashimotos]]. See [[oral-microbiome]].

## Key Metabolites

Indolelactate is the only gut metabolite causally associated with autoimmune hypothyroidism after FDR correction (OR=1.592, MR evidence). Tryptophan levels are significantly lower in HT (p<0.0001); supplementation alleviates thyroid damage and rebalances T cell subsets via the IDO1-Kyn-AhR axis and PI3K-Akt pathway suppression. Methylmalonic acid is elevated. Phospholipid and sphingolipid metabolism is disrupted, with altered phosphatidylcholine species in follicular fluid affecting fertility. [[butyrate]] and valeric acid are significantly decreased (p<0.01). See [[inflammation]] and [[nf-kappa-b]]. <!-- UNSOURCED: needs attribution -->

**Kynurenine pathway (2026 update).** In 57 young women with autoimmune thyroiditis and 38 controls, serum tryptophan did not differ, but [[kynurenine]] and anthranilic acid were higher and kynurenic acid lower [[krupa-2024-kynurenine-pathway-metabolites-autoimmune-thyroiditis]]. The anthranilic-to-kynurenic acid ratio separated the groups (AUC 0.729), and [[kynurenine-pathway]] metabolites together with FT4 explained 56.5% of the variance in peripheral deiodinase activity, which was lower in autoimmune thyroiditis [[krupa-2024-kynurenine-pathway-metabolites-autoimmune-thyroiditis]]. This conflicts with the lower tryptophan reported above, suggesting the pathway may be redirected in HT without total tryptophan necessarily falling [[krupa-2024-kynurenine-pathway-metabolites-autoimmune-thyroiditis]].

**Genetically predicted metabolites.** In Mendelian randomization (genetic inference, not clinical), 3-indoleglyoxylic acid, a tryptophan-derived indole, was associated with autoimmune thyroid disease as a whole (OR 1.09, 95% CI 1.05 to 1.14, FDR 0.009) but not specifically with HT [[zhang-2024-gut-microbiota-metabolites-aitd-mendelian-randomization]]. Taurocholic acid, a conjugated bile acid, showed a nominal association with HT that strengthened after adjusting for BMI (OR 1.21, 95% CI 1.08 to 1.35) [[zhang-2024-gut-microbiota-metabolites-aitd-mendelian-randomization]]. Note that this study tested a different metabolite panel from the indolelactate analysis above, so the two results do not directly contradict each other [[zhang-2024-gut-microbiota-metabolites-aitd-mendelian-randomization]].

**Bile acids and lipid mediators.** In first-trimester pregnant women with euthyroid autoimmune thyroiditis (26 cases, 30 controls), gut diversity did not differ, but fecal arachidonic acid, several prostaglandins, deoxycholic acid and chenodeoxycholic acid were lower [[chen-2026-gut-microbiota-fecal-metabolites-euthyroid-ait-early-pregnancy]]. Fecal sulfoacetic acid correlated with TPO antibodies (r=0.623, P<0.001), and deoxycholic acid correlated inversely with them (r=-0.470) [[chen-2026-gut-microbiota-fecal-metabolites-euthyroid-ait-early-pregnancy]]. Microbial genes for bile acid metabolism were predicted to be lower in hypothyroid HT, an inference from 16S data rather than a measurement [[li-2026-thyroid-functional-state-gut-dysbiosis-hashimotos]].

**Short-chain fatty acids.** A narrative review states that propionate and butyrate are reduced in autoimmune thyroid disease, but gives no citation for that specific claim [[yan-2024-gut-microbiota-metabolites-aitd-review]]. Its evidence that butyrate expands Tregs comes from mouse models and cell studies (non-clinical) [[yan-2024-gut-microbiota-metabolites-aitd-review]]. No study in this batch measured fecal or serum SCFAs in people with HT.

## Diet and Probiotics

AIP diet pilot (n=16): significant QoL improvement across all SF-36 subscales and 29% hs-CRP reduction, but no change in thyroid antibodies or hormones. Mediterranean diet traits are protective; meat consumption increases HT odds via AGE accumulation and selenoenzyme suppression. Selenium 200 ug/day + vitamin D supplementation is the best-evidenced mineral intervention. Gluten-free diet alters the microbiome but may paradoxically increase Desulfobacterota/Proteobacteria without improving thyroid markers. Fiber intake of 30g/day supports SCFA production. <!-- UNSOURCED: needs attribution -->

[[probiotics|Probiotic]] meta-analysis (9 RCTs, 395 participants) shows significant TSH reduction (SMD: -1.10) and increased free T3/T4. Probiotics alone outperform synbiotics for TSH reduction. Shorter interventions (<=8 weeks) show stronger effects. B. longum + methimazole synergy demonstrated for [[graves-disease]]. Strain-specific effects and high heterogeneity across trials mean probiotic prescriptions remain evolving. <!-- UNSOURCED: needs attribution -->

**Vitamin D.** In a meta-analysis of case-control studies, the HT subgroup (6 studies) had lower 25(OH)D (SMD -1.13, 95% CI -1.64 to -0.62) and higher odds of deficiency (OR 4.07, 95% CI 2.12 to 7.82), but heterogeneity was very high (I2 = 93.5%) and the design cannot establish cause [[wang-2015-vitamin-d-autoimmune-thyroid-disease-meta-analysis]]. A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (n=652) found that [[vitamin-d]] lowered TPO antibodies (SMD -1.11, 95% CI -1.92 to -0.29) and Tg antibodies (SMD -1.12), with the effect confined to treatment longer than 3 months [[zhang-2021-vitamin-d-thyroid-autoimmunity-hashimotos-meta-analysis]]. Only three trials used a blinded placebo, heterogeneity exceeded 90%, and TSH and free T4 were not pooled [[zhang-2021-vitamin-d-thyroid-autoimmunity-hashimotos-meta-analysis]].

**Selenium.** The largest synthesis, of 35 RCTs, found that [[selenium]] lowered TPO antibodies (SMD -0.96, 95% CI -1.36 to -0.56; GRADE low certainty) and lowered TSH in patients not taking thyroid hormone (SMD -0.21), with no clear effect on Tg antibodies, free T4, free T3 or thyroid volume and no rise in adverse events (OR 0.89) [[huwiler-2024-selenium-supplementation-hashimoto-meta-analysis]]. In an open-label randomized trial of 90 untreated patients, 200 ug/day selenium yeast for 6 months produced larger falls in TPO and Tg antibodies than no treatment, but absolute antibody levels at 6 months did not differ between groups; activated regulatory T cells were higher with selenium (13.19 vs 11.49%, p=0.012) [[hu-2021-selenium-thyroid-autoimmunity-tregs-hashimotos-rct]]. A single-arm study of myo-inositol 600 mg with selenomethionine 83 ug in 87 patients reported TSH falling from 4.32 to 3.12 mIU/L over 6 months, but with no control group, regression to the mean cannot be excluded [[nordio-2017-myo-inositol-selenium-autoimmune-thyroiditis]].

**Gluten-free diet.** In an open-label trial that randomized women with HT and no celiac disease, 12 months on a [[gluten-free-diet]] did not change TPO or Tg antibodies compared with controls, although TSH was slightly lower (1.44 vs 1.93 uIU/mL, p=0.044) [[poblocki-2021-gluten-free-diet-chronic-autoimmune-thyroiditis]]. A meta-analysis of four small single-arm studies (n=87) found borderline, non-significant falls in TPO antibodies (g -0.40, p=0.07) and Tg antibodies (g -0.39, p=0.06), and small improvements in TSH and free T4 [[piticchio-2023-gluten-free-diet-autoimmune-thyroiditis-meta-analysis]]. The antibody effect was driven by participants with gluten-related conditions such as non-celiac gluten sensitivity [[piticchio-2023-gluten-free-diet-autoimmune-thyroiditis-meta-analysis]]. Together these do not support routine gluten avoidance in HT without [[celiac-disease]] or gluten sensitivity [[poblocki-2021-gluten-free-diet-chronic-autoimmune-thyroiditis]] [[piticchio-2023-gluten-free-diet-autoimmune-thyroiditis-meta-analysis]].

**Probiotics.** No open-access probiotic RCT in HT was available in this batch, so the probiotic evidence above is unchanged.

## Comorbidities

81% of HT patients have [[comorbidities]]: thyroid nodules (53%), breast hyperplasia (47%), uterine leiomyomas (24%). Female patients are 6.2x more likely to have comorbidities than males. Levothyroxine therapy reduces comorbidity risk by correcting TSH-driven tissue overstimulation.

- [[graves-disease]] -- sister AITD with shared genetics (HLA-DRB1, PTPN22) and mineral dependencies; patients can convert between HT and GD; both feature gut-thyroid axis disruption, Se dependence, and SCFA producer depletion
- [[depression]] -- depression occurs even in euthyroid HT through neuroinflammation mechanisms; shared tryptophan pathway disruption (tryptophan significantly lower in HT, p<0.0001); shared gut-brain axis disruption and SCFA depletion
- [[celiac-disease]] -- celiac prevalence is 2-5x higher in HT than general population; shared autoimmune architecture and mucosal immune dysregulation; gluten-driven intestinal inflammation may trigger thyroid autoimmunity via molecular mimicry; both improve partially with gluten-free diet in some patients
