# Fannyhessea vaginae

*Fannyhessea vaginae* is the current name for NCBITaxon:82135. The organism was originally described as *Atopobium vaginae*, which remains its validly published basionym and the name used by much of the clinical literature [[ncbi-taxonomy-fannyhessea-vaginae]] [[lpsn-fannyhessea-vaginae]].

## Nomenclatural History

Rodriguez Jovita and colleagues described the species from a human vaginal isolate using phenotypic testing, whole-cell protein analysis, and 16S rRNA phylogeny; their type strain CCUG 38953 is also deposited as ATCC BAA-55 and DSM 15829 [[rodriguez-jovita-1999-atopobium-vaginae-description]]. Nouioui and colleagues later transferred the species to the new genus *Fannyhessea* in their genome-based classification of Actinobacteria [[nouioui-2018-actinobacteria-taxonomic-classification]]. NCBI and LPSN both apply *Fannyhessea vaginae* as the current name [[ncbi-taxonomy-fannyhessea-vaginae]] [[lpsn-fannyhessea-vaginae]].

## Bacterial Vaginosis Evidence

A culture-independent study published under the historical name *Atopobium vaginae* found the organism abundant in disturbed vaginal microbiota and strongly associated with bacterial vaginosis, often alongside *Gardnerella vaginalis* [[verhelst-2004-atopobium-vaginae-bacterial-vaginosis]]. This association does not by itself establish that *F. vaginae* causes every case, that it translocates from the gut, or that it drives pelvic inflammatory disease or adverse pregnancy outcomes.
