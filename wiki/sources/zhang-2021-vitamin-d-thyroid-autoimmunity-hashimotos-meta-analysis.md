# Effects of vitamin D on thyroid autoimmunity markers in Hashimoto's thyroiditis: systematic review and meta-analysis

## Key Findings

- **Vitamin D supplementation lowered TPOAb titre** vs control: SMD -1.11 (95% CI -1.92 to -0.29), P = 0.008, 8 RCTs, n = 652, I2 = 95.3% (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Vitamin D supplementation lowered TGAb titre**: SMD -1.12 (95% CI -1.96 to -0.28), P = 0.009, 6 RCTs, I2 = 93.3% (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Duration mattered**: treatment longer than 3 months reduced TPOAb (SMD -1.66, 95% CI -2.91 to -0.41, P = 0.009); treatment of 3 months or less did not (SMD -0.16, 95% CI -0.51 to 0.20, P = 0.392) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Form mattered (as categorized by the authors)**: "vitamin D3" reduced TPOAb (SMD -1.48, 95% CI -2.53 to -0.42, P = 0.006); "vitamin D" did not (SMD -0.01, 95% CI -0.35 to 0.32, P = 0.945) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Heterogeneity was very high and trial quality mixed**: only 3 of 8 trials used dummy placebos with blinding; most trials were Chinese; thyroid function outcomes (TSH, FT4) were not pooled (systematic-review-meta-analysis)

## Methods

- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials, PRISMA-guided, registered in PROSPERO (CRD42020206000).
- **Search**: PubMed, MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, CNKI, from inception to August 2020; no language restrictions; reference lists hand-searched. 345 records, 153 duplicates removed, 24 full texts assessed, 8 RCTs included.
- **Eligibility**: RCTs in HT patients receiving vitamin D at any dose or duration, reporting TPOAb and/or TGAb.
- **Included trials (Table 1)**: Han 2015 (China, n=62), Simsek 2016 (Turkey, n=82; control "sunshine and diet"), Zhao 2016 (China, n=36), Vahabi Anaraki 2017 (Iran, n=65, placebo), Knutsen 2017 ("Middle East, Africa, or South Asia" as printed, n=165, placebo), Chahardoli 2019 (Iran, n=40, women only, placebo), Yin 2019 (China, n=100), Jia 2020 (China, n=102). Four Chinese trials compared vitamin D plus levothyroxine (LT4) vs LT4. Durations 4 to 24 weeks. Mean ages roughly 35 to 46 years; female proportion 47% to 100%.
- **Doses as printed**: "25 mg QD" (as printed in the table; units appear to be a typesetting issue), 1000 IU daily, 50000 IU weekly.
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane tool. 3 trials described random sequence generation; 3 described allocation concealment; 3 used dummy placebos with blinding; 3 had losses to follow-up (high risk for incomplete outcome data).
- **Analysis**: SMD with 95% CI; random effects when I2 > 50%; subgroup analysis by duration and form; leave-one-out sensitivity; Begg's test for publication bias (P < 0.10). Stata 14.0.

## Detailed Summary

- **TPOAb (8 RCTs)**: chi2 = 150.02, I2 = 95.3%, P < 0.001. Random-effects SMD -1.11 (95% CI -1.92 to -0.29), P = 0.008. (The abstract prints the CI as "1-1.92, -0.29", a typographical error; the results text gives -1.92 to -0.29.)
- **TPOAb by duration**: >3 months SMD -1.66 (95% CI -2.91 to -0.41), P = 0.009; 3 months or less SMD -0.16 (95% CI -0.51 to 0.20), P = 0.392. Derived from Table 1: trials longer than 3 months were Han, Zhao, Yin, Jia (all Chinese LT4 co-treatment) and Knutsen; trials of 3 months or less were Simsek, Vahabi Anaraki, and Chahardoli. Duration is therefore partly confounded with country and LT4 co-treatment.
- **TPOAb by form**: vitamin D3 SMD -1.48 (95% CI -2.53 to -0.42), P = 0.006; vitamin D SMD -0.01 (95% CI -0.35 to 0.32), P = 0.945. Subgroup membership is shown only in Figure 4B; Table 1 labels most "D3" arms as 1,25(OH)2D3.
- **TGAb (6 RCTs)**: chi2 = 75.12, I2 = 93.3%, P < 0.001. SMD -1.12 (95% CI -1.96 to -0.28), P = 0.009. No subgroup analysis due to limited data.
- **Sensitivity**: leave-one-out estimates all fell within the 95% CI of the overall effect.
- **Publication bias**: Begg's test TPOAb Z = 1.61, Pr > |Z| = 0.108; TGAb Z = 0.00, Pr > |Z| = 1.000.
- **Not pooled**: TSH, FT3, FT4, 25(OH)D change, adverse events, or clinical outcomes.
- **Mechanistic discussion (authors' interpretation, not tested)**: 1,25(OH)2D3 downregulates MHC class II and costimulatory molecules, increases IL-10, reduces IL-12, represses Th1 responses, inhibits plasma cell generation, and supports regulatory B cells.

### Author-stated limitations

1. Limited number of studies and sample sizes restrict power.
2. Significant heterogeneity among studies.
3. Most included studies were conducted in China, limiting generalizability.
4. Only published data were included, possibly overestimating the effect.

## Relevance to WikiBiome

- Informs [[vitamin-d]] and [[hashimotos-thyroiditis]] intervention evidence: vitamin D supplementation is associated with lower thyroid autoantibody titres in pooled RCTs.
- Relevant to [[thyroid-autoimmunity]] as an antibody-level (surrogate) outcome; no microbiome data.
- Complements the observational finding of lower 25(OH)D in HT in [[wang-2015-vitamin-d-autoimmune-thyroid-disease-meta-analysis]].

## Relevance to Cureva

- Intervention hypothesis: vitamin D (especially D3, courses longer than 3 months) may lower TPOAb and TGAb in HT. Effect is on surrogate antibody markers only; no hypothyroidism-progression or symptom outcomes were pooled.
- Caution: the positive pooled effect is driven largely by open-label Chinese LT4 co-treatment trials; the three placebo-controlled trials were in the 3 months or less subgroup or Knutsen, and the short-duration subgroup was null.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- Very high heterogeneity (I2 above 93%) means the pooled SMD may not represent a consistent effect.
- The duration and form subgroups overlap with trial setting (Chinese, LT4 co-treatment, unblinded), so it is unclear whether duration, compound, or trial quality explains the difference.
- Calcitriol (1,25(OH)2D3) is the active hormone, not a nutritional supplement; the table labels several "D3" arms as 1,25(OH)2D3, which complicates translation to cholecalciferol dosing.

## Open Questions

- Does cholecalciferol (not calcitriol) at defined doses reduce TPOAb in blinded, placebo-controlled trials longer than 3 months?
- Does antibody reduction translate into slower progression to hypothyroidism or reduced LT4 requirement?
- Does baseline 25(OH)D status modify the effect?
- Are there interactions with selenium status or gut microbiome composition?
