# Meta-Analysis of the Association between Vitamin D and Autoimmune Thyroid Disease

## Key Findings

- **Autoimmune thyroid disease (AITD) patients had lower serum 25(OH)D than healthy controls**: SMD -0.99 (95% CI -1.31 to -0.66), 19 studies, 1782 cases vs 1821 controls, I2 = 94.8%, Egger's p = 0.009 (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **AITD patients were more likely to be 25(OH)D deficient**: OR 2.99 (95% CI 1.88 to 4.74), 9 studies, 994 cases vs 1035 controls, I2 = 73.0%, Egger's p = 0.056 (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Hashimoto's thyroiditis (HT) subgroup**: lower 25(OH)D (SMD -1.13, 95% CI -1.64 to -0.62; 6 studies; I2 = 93.5%) and higher odds of deficiency (OR 4.07, 95% CI 2.12 to 7.82; 3 studies; I2 = 52.5%, heterogeneity p = 0.122) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Graves' disease subgroup**: lower 25(OH)D (SMD -1.04, 95% CI -1.52 to -0.57; 13 studies; I2 = 95.3%) and OR for deficiency 3.50 (95% CI 1.86 to 6.56; 6 studies; I2 = 66.9%) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Causality not addressed**: all pooled studies were case-control; the authors state prospective studies are needed to determine whether low vitamin D is a cause or a consequence of AITD (systematic-review-meta-analysis)

## Methods

- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis of case-control studies comparing serum 25(OH)D between AITD patients (HT and Graves' disease) and healthy controls.
- **Search**: PubMed, Embase, CENTRAL, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), updated to 20 December 2014; English or Chinese only; no hand-searching.
- **Eligibility**: population-based case-control design; AITD cases vs healthy controls; quantitative (mean plus or minus SD) and/or qualitative (deficiency) vitamin D data; Newcastle-Ottawa Scale score of 7 or higher.
- **Included**: 431 unique references screened; 20 articles included (19 with continuous data, 9 with dichotomous deficiency data). Two of the included items are Chinese master's dissertations (Huang ZL 2013, Liu XH 2012, per reference list).
- **Populations**: study-level AITD sample sizes ranged from 26 to 360 cases; mean ages roughly 32 to 49 years; mostly female (several studies all-female). Country of each study is not reported in the tables.
- **Assays**: ELISA, HPLC, ECLIA, CPBA, RIA, DCCLIA; seasons of sampling varied. nmol/L values converted to ng/mL (divided by 2.496).
- **Deficiency cut-offs**: varied across studies (<10, <15, or <20 ng/mL).
- **Analysis**: random-effects SMD (continuous) and OR (dichotomous); heterogeneity by Q-test and I2; publication bias by Egger's test (p < 0.1 considered bias); Stata 12.0.

## Detailed Summary

### Overall AITD vs controls

- Continuous 25(OH)D: SMD -0.99 (95% CI -1.31 to -0.66), I2 94.8%, p < 0.01; 3603 participants. Egger's test p = 0.009 (publication bias).
- Deficiency: OR 2.99 (95% CI 1.88 to 4.74), I2 73.0%; 2029 participants. Egger's test p = 0.056 (publication bias by the authors' p < 0.1 threshold).
- Not all individual studies agreed: Jyotsna 2012 (SMD 0.25, 95% CI -0.06 to 0.56), Effraimidis 2012 (SMD 0.04, -0.30 to 0.38), and Wang ZS 2014 (SMD 1.15, 0.65 to 1.64, i.e. higher vitamin D in AITD) did not show lower vitamin D in cases. Two studies had extreme effects (Huang ZL 2013 SMD -5.52; Han Y 2013 SMD -5.95).

### Hashimoto's thyroiditis subgroup

- Continuous: SMD -1.13 (95% CI -1.64 to -0.62), I2 93.5%; 6 studies (Tamer 2011, Liu 2012, Huang 2013, Bozkurt 2013, Mansournia 2014, Xuan 2014). Study SMDs ranged from -0.46 to -5.52.
- Deficiency: OR 4.07 (95% CI 2.12 to 7.82), I2 52.5% (p = 0.122); 3 studies (Kivity 2011 OR 8.31, Bozkurt 2013 OR 2.76, Mansournia 2014 OR 4.25).
- Total HT participant numbers for the subgroup were not reported in the text.

### Graves' disease subgroup

- Continuous: SMD -1.04 (95% CI -1.52 to -0.57), I2 95.3%; 13 studies.
- Deficiency: OR 3.50 (95% CI 1.86 to 6.56), I2 66.9% (p = 0.010); 6 studies.

### Mechanistic discussion (authors' interpretation, not tested in this analysis)

The authors link vitamin D to regulation of Th1, Th2, and Th17 cells and secretion of IFN-gamma, IL-4, and IL-17, citing HT as Th1-dominant. They mention one small RCT in which vitamin D reduced TPOAb and TgAb vs placebo, but state evidence for supplementation is not definitive.

### Author-stated limitations

- Many primary studies did not adjust for confounders such as season or assay method.
- Inter-assay and inter-laboratory variability; deficiency cut-offs differed; AITD diagnostic methods differed (thyroid function tests, antibodies, with or without ultrasound).
- Language differences and publication bias may contribute to heterogeneity.
- Case-control design cannot establish whether low vitamin D is causal or a consequence.
- Statistical heterogeneity was substantial; the authors judged clinical heterogeneity not major and pooling appropriate.

## Relevance to Cureva

- Provides observational rationale for checking 25(OH)D status in HT patients; does not by itself support supplementation for thyroid outcomes (see [[zhang-2021-vitamin-d-thyroid-autoimmunity-hashimotos-meta-analysis]] for RCT pooling).
- Evidence grade for "low vitamin D in HT" is association-level from case-control studies with very high heterogeneity (I2 above 90% for continuous outcomes).

## Contradictions / Tensions

- Three of 19 continuous-data studies did not find lower vitamin D in AITD, and one found higher levels; pooled effect is driven partly by two extreme Chinese studies (SMD below -5).
- Egger's test indicated publication bias for the continuous analysis (p = 0.009) yet the authors still judged pooling appropriate.
- Internal inconsistency: Table 2 lists Mansournia 2014 with p = 0.82 for deficiency, but the forest plots give OR 4.25 (95% CI 1.56 to 11.58).
- The search excluded languages other than English and Chinese; eligibility required "population-based" case-control studies, but two included items are master's dissertations.

## Open Questions

- Is low 25(OH)D a cause or a consequence of HT (reverse causation, reduced sun exposure, illness behavior)?
- Do results hold after adjustment for season, latitude, BMI, and assay standardization?
- Does vitamin D status interact with gut microbiome composition in HT patients?
- Would Mendelian randomization or prospective cohort data confirm the association?
