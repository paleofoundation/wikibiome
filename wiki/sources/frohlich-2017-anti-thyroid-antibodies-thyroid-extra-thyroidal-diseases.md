# Thyroid Autoimmunity: Role of Anti-thyroid Antibodies in Thyroid and Extra-Thyroidal Diseases

## Key Findings

- **Anti-TPO is the most sensitive marker:** detected in 90 to 95% of AITD patients, 80% of Graves' disease (GD), and 10 to 15% of non-AITD patients; more common and more indicative of thyroid disease than anti-Tg (expert-opinion)
- **Anti-Tg is less specific:** about 10% of healthy young subjects and 15% of people >60 years; 60 to 80% of HT and 50 to 60% of GD patients (another cited source: 70 to 80% of AITD, 30 to 40% of GD, 10 to 15% of non-thyroid immune disorders) (expert-opinion)
- **Functional roles differ:** HT anti-TPO can fix complement, destroy thyrocytes and competitively inhibit TPO, but its contribution to damage is minor versus T-cell and cytokine-mediated apoptosis; anti-Tg does not fix complement (epitopes too widely spaced) and has no defined action (expert-opinion)
- **Titers persist despite treatment:** on levothyroxine, anti-TPO falls only slowly (e.g., over 5 years) and stays in the pathological range; no correlation of titer with risk of hypothyroidism has been found (expert-opinion)
- **Extra-thyroidal associations are mostly correlational:** Hashimoto's encephalopathy, miscarriage and preterm delivery in IVF/ICSI, vasculo-placental complications, lower frailty in older women, and conflicting breast-cancer links (expert-opinion)

## Methods

- **Design:** narrative review (no systematic search, no pooling). Authors from University of Tuebingen and Medical University Graz. No funding.
- **Scope:** etiology and mechanisms of autoimmune thyroid disease (AITD); prevalence and properties of anti-TPO, anti-Tg and anti-TSHR antibodies; extra-thyroidal effects; AITD and cancer.
- **Authors' caveats:** assays differ in specificity and sensitivity; thyroid hormone changes confound; few prospective studies.

## Detailed Summary

**Antibody comparison (Table 1, summarized):**

| | Anti-TSHR | Anti-TPO | Anti-Tg |
|---|---|---|---|
| Antigen location | Extracellular | Intracellular | Intrafollicular, low levels in blood |
| Immune access | Without tissue destruction | After thyrocyte destruction | With and without destruction |
| Class | Mainly IgG1 | IgG1, IgG4 > IgG2, IgG3; low IgA | IgG1, IgG4 > IgG2, IgG3; low IgA and IgM |
| Prevalence in AITD | ~90% GD; ~10% HT | >80% in GD and HT | >50% in GD and HT |
| Other autoimmune disease | Usually absent (one study 18% in T1DM) | 16 to 37% RA; 40% T1DM; 12 to 30% celiac | 12 to 23% RA; 30% T1DM; 11 to 32% celiac |
| Action | Stimulating, blocking, apoptosis | Little action per se | No defined action |

**Anti-TPO:** IgG subclass prevalence reported as IgG1 70%, IgG4 66.1%, IgG2 35.1%, IgG3 19.6%. Antibodies from healthy subjects and patients recognize the same epitopes, but only patient antibodies are reported to fix complement and inhibit enzyme activity. Anti-TPO associated with oxidative stress markers. Approximate positivity thresholds "according to most laboratories": >35 U/l anti-TPO, >20 U/l anti-Tg, >1.75 U/ml anti-TSHR (assays not directly comparable).

**Anti-Tg:** polyclonal in healthy subjects, oligoclonal in AITD. IgG4 dominant in GD and IgG2 in HT (one study). IgM anti-Tg about 1% in healthy individuals.

**Iodine:** iodization increased population anti-TPO prevalence from 14.3 to 23.8% and anti-Tg from 13.7 to 19.9%; iodine administration induced antibodies in 8 to 20% of subjects; excessive salt iodine (40 to 100 mg/kg for 5 years) increased thyroid autoimmunity; high oral intake (~400 to 600 ug/day) may increase Tg immunogenicity.

**Other modifiers:** smoking lowers anti-Tg and anti-TPO and protects against HT while favoring GD (OR 3.3); alcohol abstainers have 2.17 higher risk of GD and HT; selenium supplementation does not consistently lower anti-TPO; vitamin D data inconclusive. Pregnancy transiently lowers antibodies with rebound about 6 weeks postpartum. Twin data: antibody formation correlated at 73% in twins.

**Gut and infection:** the review states gut microbiota "can trigger HT" and enteroviruses have been found in thyroid tissue, yet also that the role of commensal bacteria in HT "is not yet proven"; Yersinia enterocolitica cross-reactivity with TSHR (molecular mimicry) is proposed for GD; leaky gut may raise risk but needs study; H. pylori and HTLV-1 linked to AITD.

**Other autoimmune diseases:** anti-TPO 37% and anti-Tg 22.9% in RA vs 13% and 11.5% in healthy controls (one study); T1DM anti-TPO 39.6% and anti-Tg 30% (one study; others 7 to 40% and ~15%); celiac disease anti-TPO 11.7 to 30.5% and anti-Tg 11.2 to 32%, with decreases reported after GFD; asthma 27.3% and 15% vs 10% and 8.3% controls.

**Extra-thyroidal (anti-TPO):** Hashimoto's encephalopathy (anti-TPO common feature; alpha-enolase proposed as true antigen); vestibular dysfunction; increased miscarriage and preterm delivery in antibody-positive women undergoing IVF/ICSI without differences in oocyte retrieval or IVF success; suspected vasculo-placental complications; impaired thyroid response to hCG. Anti-TPO-positive older women were less frail.

**Cancer:** breast cancer prevalence 6.11% vs 2.07% in thyroid disease vs controls in one study, but only 6 of 13 studies found an association (pooled RR 1.07 in Sarlis et al.). Anti-TPO-positive breast cancer patients had better survival in some studies; findings conflicting. HT coexistence with thyroid cancer reported at 1 to 23%.

## Relevance to Cureva

- Antibody titers fall slowly and do not track hypothyroidism risk, so short trials using anti-TPO/anti-Tg as primary endpoints (e.g., GFD, selenium) should be interpreted cautiously.
- Iodine excess is a documented promoter of thyroid autoimmunity; selenium effects on anti-TPO are inconsistent.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- Internal tension on the microbiome: "Gut microbiota can trigger HT" versus "The role of commensal bacteria to the development of HT is not yet proven."
- The summary sentence on anti-Tg states independent effects "have been identified so far", which conflicts with Table 1 ("No defined action") and appears to be a missing "not".
- Prevalence figures for anti-Tg vary by cited source (60 to 80% vs 70 to 80% in HT/AITD), reflecting assay differences.

## Open Questions

- Do anti-TPO or anti-Tg have causal roles outside the thyroid, or are they markers of immune activation?
- Which environmental triggers (iodine, microbiota, infection) are causally upstream of antibody formation in HT?
- How should antibody endpoints be standardized across assays for intervention trials?
