# Oral Microbiota Signatures in the Pathogenesis of Euthyroid Hashimoto's Thyroiditis

## Key Findings

- **No differences in salivary alpha diversity** (Shannon, Simpson) or beta diversity (PCoA) between euthyroid HT patients and healthy controls (case-control, n=78)
- **Phylum Patescibacteria was more abundant in HT saliva** than in controls (3.59 vs. 1.12; p=0.022), the only dominant phylum to differ (case-control, n=78)
- **Four genera were enriched in HT saliva**: Gemella (0.71 vs. 0.11; p<0.001), Enterococcus (0.099 vs. 0.014; p<0.0001), Staphylococcus (0.08 vs. 0.01; p<0.0001) and Bacillus (0.07 vs. 0.007; p<0.0001); authors summarize approximately 7, 9 and 10-fold increases for Gemella, Enterococcus and Bacillus (case-control, n=78)
- **Levothyroxine status did not change the oral microbiota**: no significant differences between treated (LT+HT) and untreated (LT-HT) euthyroid HT subgroups (case-control, n=60 HT)
- Twelve phyla and 163 genera differed at p<0.05 between HT and controls, with no multiple-testing correction reported (case-control, n=78)

## Methods

- **Design**: Single-center study described by the authors as cross-sectional observational; structurally a patient vs. age- and sex-matched healthy control comparison (case-control by repo convention).
- **Setting**: Istanbul, Turkey; ethics approval Istinye University (2/2021.K-43, 18.06.2021). Funded by Beykent University (2021-2022-BAP-02).
- **Population**: 78 women. 60 euthyroid HT patients (diagnosis by serum TPOAb and heterogeneous, coarsened parenchyma on ultrasound): 32 without levothyroxine (LT-HT) and 28 on levothyroxine (LT+HT); 18 healthy controls (HC).
- **Baseline (mean +/- SD; LT-HT, LT+HT, HC)**: age 39.4 +/- 4.52, 39.8 +/- 6.04, 39.1 +/- 5.8 years (p=0.893); BMI 21.6 +/- 2.1, 22.4 +/- 1.9, 20.8 +/- 2.3 kg/m2 (p=0.914); TSH 1.86 +/- 1.81, 2.12 +/- 1.94, 1.61 +/- 1.35 mU/L (p=0.785); TPOAb 265 +/- 224, 342 +/- 274, 7.8 +/- 4.9 IU/mL (p<0.0001).
- **Exclusions**: salivary gland surgery, pregnancy, fixed orthodontics, antibiotics/anti-inflammatories/anticoagulants/probiotics within 4 weeks, age <18, active infection, active oral disease.
- **Sampling**: Unstimulated saliva (>=5 mL) after tooth brushing at least 1 h prior, fasting from food, drink and cigarettes, and 1 min 0.9% saline rinse; DNA/RNA Shield; stored at -80 C.
- **Sequencing**: 16S rRNA V3-V4, Illumina MiSeq 2x300 bp; Zymo Quick-DNA Fecal/Soil Microbe kit; Trimmomatic v0.39; Kraken2 against SILVA (2022); R vegan for diversity; Kruskal-Wallis tests; p<0.05.

## Detailed Summary

- Mean 50,305 reads per sample (SD 6,756; range 43,549 to 57,061); reads clustered into 70 phyla, 162 classes, 372 orders, 583 families and 1,496 genera at 97% similarity.
- Alpha diversity (mean +/- SD; LT-HT, LT+HT, HC): Simpson 0.89 +/- 0.04, 0.91 +/- 0.03, 0.87 +/- 0.05 (p=0.952); Shannon 3.71 +/- 2.12, 3.68 +/- 1.94, 3.73 +/- 2.03 (p=0.874).
- **Phylum relative abundance, HT vs. HC**: Firmicutes 39.52 vs. 33.93; Bacteroidota 17.38 vs. 12.57; Actinobacteriota 13.4 vs. 10.19; Proteobacteria 4.05 vs. 17.71; Patescibacteria 3.59 vs. 1.12 (p=0.022, the only significant one); Fusobacteriota 2.78 vs. 2.6; Spirochaetota 0.25 vs. 0.13; Campylobacterota 0.14 vs. 0.42.
- **Top genera, HT vs. HC**: Streptococcus 3.42 vs. 2.16; Prevotella_7 1.3 vs. 1.46; Porphyromonas 1.24 vs. 0.24; Actinomyces 0.79 vs. 0.84; Gemella 0.71 vs. 0.11 (p<0.001, only significant top-10 genus); Rothia 0.68 vs. 0.24; Peptostreptococcus 0.44 vs. 0.1; Veillonella 0.44 vs. 1.4; Prevotella 0.43 vs. 0.46; Leptotrichia 0.36 vs. 0.18.
- **Subgroup vs. HC**: LT-HT vs. HC: Gemella 0.75 vs. 0.1 (p=0.004), Enterococcus 0.098 vs. 0.014 (p<0.0001), Staphylococcus 0.097 vs. 0.013 (p<0.0001), Bacillus 0.083 vs. 0.007 (p=0.004). LT+HT vs. HC: Gemella 0.67 vs. 0.1 (p=0.048), Enterococcus 0.1 vs. 0.014 (p=0.02), Bacillus 0.066 vs. 0.007 (p=0.004); Staphylococcus 0.065 vs. 0.013 (p=0.07, not significant).
- **Internal inconsistencies**: the Discussion says Faecalibacterium, Bacteroides, Prevotella_9 and Lachnoclostridium were higher in HT saliva, then calls a Faecalibacterium decrease "striking"; the comparison table (Table 3) for "this study" lists Bacillus among decreased taxa although Results report Bacillus as increased. Rely on Results values.
- **Author-stated limitations**: single center; no metabolomic or transcriptomic validation (funding); short-term follow-up; no data on disease history. Additional limitations not stated by authors: small control group (n=18), women only, no multiple-testing correction across 163 genera, oral health/periodontal status not quantified beyond exclusion of active disease.

## Relevance to Cureva

- Signature-level observation only; no intervention tested. Levothyroxine did not alter salivary composition in this sample, which suggests oral findings are not drug artifacts (low confidence given n=28 vs 32).
- Enrichment of facultative Gram-positive genera (Enterococcus, Staphylococcus, Bacillus, Gemella) could feed a primitive 5 "suppress pathobionts" hypothesis; needs replication before any clinical use.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- Diverges from gut-based HT studies (e.g. Ishaq, Liu, Zhao, cited by the authors) on phylum direction; authors attribute this to oral vs. gut niches. Proteobacteria appear lower in HT saliva (4.05 vs. 17.71, not significant).
- Authors note Wang et al. (pregnant women with hypothyroidism) found decreased oral Firmicutes and Actinobacteria, opposite to the non-significant increases here.

## Open Questions

- Do Patescibacteria (candidate phyla radiation, ultrasmall epibionts) and Gemella enrichment replicate in larger, multi-center HT cohorts with corrected statistics?
- Are these oral changes cause or consequence of thyroid autoimmunity (authors explicitly leave this open)?
- How do salivary findings relate to periodontal status and to gut composition in the same individuals?
