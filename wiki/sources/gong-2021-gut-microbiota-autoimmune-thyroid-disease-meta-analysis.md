# Association Between Gut Microbiota and Autoimmune Thyroid Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Key Findings

- **Richness diverges by AITD subtype.** Chao1 was higher in HT than controls (SMD 0.68, 95% CI 0.16 to 1.20) and lower in Graves' disease (SMD -0.87, 95% CI -1.46 to -0.28); random-effects subgroup analysis of 4 studies, I2=0% (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus lower in AITD.** Fixed-effects SMD -1.08 (95% CI -1.54 to -0.63; Z=4.66) and -0.86 (95% CI -1.31 to -0.41; Z=3.78); these pool HT and Graves' together (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Bacteroides fragilis higher in AITD**: 0.6% (95% CI 0.002 to 0.011) vs 0.1% (0.001 to 0.002); in HT specifically 0.6% vs 0.4% (Z=2.15, P=0.031) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Phylum shifts in HT are small with wide, overlapping CIs**: Firmicutes 48.3% vs 55.1% (ratio 0.88), Bacteroidetes 41.4% vs 35.2% (ratio 1.13), Bacteroides 16.4% vs 14.5% (ratio 1.13) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Authors state the data show association, not causation**, and most included studies did not control for diet or thyroid hormone status (systematic-review-meta-analysis)

## Methods

- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis, PROSPERO CRD42021251557. Searched PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane up to August 2021.
- **Eligibility**: Human AITD vs controls with extractable gut microbiota data; English; full text; excluded animal studies, reviews, abstracts, studies with incomplete abundance data, and studies with fewer than 20 participants.
- **Selection**: 282 records, 92 duplicates removed, 175 excluded on title/abstract, 15 full texts assessed, 8 included.
- **Included studies** (Table 1): Zhao (China, HT 28/16 controls), El-Zawawy (Egypt, 20 AITD incl. 7 HT and 13 GD /30), Ishaq 2017 (China, HT 29/12), Cornejo-Pareja (Spain, 18 incl. 9 HT and 9 GD /11), Ishaq 2018 (China, GD 27/11), Jiang (China, GD 45/59), Chen (China, GD 15/14), Zhou (China, GD 14/7). Total 196 AITD (by Table 2, roughly 73 HT and 123 GD) and 160 age-matched controls. Six of 8 studies are from China.
- **Microbiology methods in included studies**: 16S high-throughput or pyrosequencing, real-time PCR, PCR-DGGE, all on feces.
- **Quality**: Newcastle-Ottawa scale, scores 5 to 8.
- **Analysis**: Stata 12. SMD for Chao1 and for Bifidobacterium/Lactobacillus relative abundance; pooled percentages for phyla/genera. Random effects when I2 >50%, otherwise fixed effects. Sensitivity analysis and Begg's test.

## Detailed Summary

### Alpha diversity (Chao1, 4 studies)
- HT vs controls: SMD 0.68 (95% CI 0.16 to 1.20).
- GD vs controls: SMD -0.87 (95% CI -1.46 to -0.28).
- The overall pooled effect across both subgroups was null (z=0.14, P=0.886); I2=0%. Because the two subtypes move in opposite directions, pooling HT with GD cancels out.

### Phylum level (random effects, I2 >50%)
- Firmicutes: HT 48.3% (95% CI 0.080 to 0.885) vs controls 55.1% (0.371 to 0.731); GD 41.9% vs 56.6%. Ratios 0.88 (HT) and 0.74 (GD).
- Bacteroidetes (6 studies): HT 41.4% (0.030 to 0.798) vs 35.2% (0.165 to 0.539); GD 52.1% vs 34.1%. Ratios 1.13 and 1.53.
- The reported Z and P values (e.g. Firmicutes HT Z=6.45, P=0.000) appear to test each pooled percentage against zero, not the patient vs control difference; the patient and control CIs overlap widely. Treat these phylum "differences" as descriptive.

### Genus level
- Bifidobacterium (AITD vs controls, fixed effects): SMD -1.08 (95% CI -1.54 to -0.63), Z=4.66, P=0.000.
- Lactobacillus (AITD vs controls, fixed effects): SMD -0.86 (95% CI -1.31 to -0.41), Z=3.78, P=0.000.
- Bacteroides (5 studies, random effects): HT 16.4% (0.056 to 0.272) vs 14.5% (0.110 to 0.181); GD 24.6% vs 18%. Ratios 1.13 (HT), 1.37 (GD).

### Family and species
- Lachnospiraceae: 24.3% (95% CI -0.026 to 0.512) vs 17.8% (0.040 to 0.316), Z=2.89, P=0.004. The HT/control ratio is reported as 30.3% vs 21.6% (1.40) and GD 12.0% vs 10.3% (1.17); these figures do not match the pooled 24.3% vs 17.8% in the text, an internal inconsistency.
- Bacteroides fragilis (4 studies, all reported higher in AITD): AITD 0.6% vs 0.1% (Z=3.34, P=0.001; heterogeneity 29.0%). HT 0.6% (0.001 to 0.012) vs 0.4% (0.000 to 0.007), Z=2.15, P=0.031. GD 0.6% vs 0.1%, Z=2.85, P=0.004.

### Correlations with antibodies (narrative, Table 3, from Jiang 2020, Cornejo 2020, Chen 2020)
- Negative with TPO-Ab: Bacteroides (r=-0.342), Dorea (r=-0.341), Faecalibacterium (r=-0.453), Synergistetes (r=-0.711), Coprococcus (r=-0.499).
- Positive with TPO-Ab: Blautia (0.365), Alistipes (0.432), Ruminococcaceae (0.408), Enterobacteriaceae (0.416), Lactobacillus (0.607 in one study).
- Most of these come from Graves' cohorts, not HT.

### Authors' stated limitations
Dietary habits not considered in most studies; thyroid hormones and autoantibodies not evaluated as influences on composition; seasonal variation (only one study reported collection season); metabolic changes of AITD could alter microbiota, so only association can be inferred.

## Relevance to Cureva

- The Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus decrease is the most cited quantitative basis for probiotic hypotheses in AITD, but the pooled estimate mixes HT and Graves' and does not test any intervention.
- Higher richness in HT argues against using "low diversity" as a generic HT marker.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- [[liu-2020-gut-microbiota-hashimotos-euthyroid-hypothyroid]] (not included here) found **lower** richness in HT, opposite to the pooled HT Chao1 increase.
- [[li-2026-thyroid-functional-state-gut-dysbiosis-hashimotos]] found Bifidobacterium **increased** in dysthyroid HT.
- Sawicka-Gutaj 2022 pooled relative abundances and found no significant HT vs control difference for Bacteroidetes, Bacteroides, Firmicutes or Bifidobacterium.

## Open Questions

- HT-specific pooled estimates for Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus are not reported separately.
- With only about 73 HT patients across 4 studies, are the HT findings robust to leave-one-out? Sensitivity analysis is mentioned but results are not shown.
- Different measurement methods (qPCR, DGGE, 16S) were pooled; method heterogeneity is not modeled.
