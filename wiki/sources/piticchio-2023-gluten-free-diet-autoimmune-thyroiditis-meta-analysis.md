# Effect of gluten-free diet on autoimmune thyroiditis progression in patients with no symptoms or histology of celiac disease: a meta-analysis

## Key Findings

- **Antibody reductions did not reach significance.** Pooled Hedges' g for TgAb -0.39 (95% CI -0.81 to +0.02; p=0.06; I2=46.98%) and for TPOAb -0.40 (95% CI -0.82 to +0.03; p=0.07; I2=47.58%) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **TSH decreased and FT4 increased** after gluten deprivation: TSH g -0.35 (95% CI -0.64 to -0.05; p=0.02; I2=0%); FT4 g +0.35 (95% CI 0.06 to 0.64; p=0.02; I2=0%) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **FT3 did not change:** g 0.05 (95% CI -0.38 to +0.48; p=0.82; I2=51%) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Heterogeneity explained by gluten-related conditions (GRC):** subgroup tests by presence of GRC resolved heterogeneity for TgAb (p=0.02), TPOAb (p=0.02) and FT3 (p=0.04); antibody reduction "mostly affects GRC patients" (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Authors' conclusion:** a possible positive effect, particularly with GRC, but evidence is "not yet sufficient" to recommend GFD to all HT patients; randomized trials needed (systematic-review-meta-analysis)

## Methods

- **Design:** systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis following MOOSE; PubMed and Scopus, English only, last search 7 February 2023. Two independent reviewers. Risk of bias via NHLBI Quality Assessment Tool for Observational Studies. Effect size Hedges' g; STATA/SE 17.0. Subgroup and meta-regression by sample size, continent, GFD duration, age, BMI, LT4 therapy and GRC.
- **Inclusion:** adult HT patients with TSH/FT4/FT3/TgAb/TPOAb measured before and after monitored GFD; no CD symptoms or with negative intestinal histology (incidental anti-tTG or NCGS allowed). Excluded CD, LT4 started or changed during GFD.
- **Yield:** 409 records, 20 full texts, 4 studies included (all prospective cohort designs per the authors; 3 European, 1 US; published 2019 to 2022).
- **Pooled population:** 87 HT patients on GFD, all female, ages 25 to 42, BMI 19.5 to 30; 47 had GRC. Mean GFD duration 5.5 months (6-month values used where available).

## Detailed Summary

- Risk of bias generally low: 12 of 14 items low in all studies; time frame of exposure rated high risk in one study (about 3 months of exclusion). No study justified sample size.
- Effects are pre/post within GFD arms; comparisons to non-GFD control arms were not pooled.
- FT3 heterogeneity was also resolved by LT4 status (taking stable LT4 vs not; p=0.03).
- The FT4 effect is printed as "+0.35%" in the abstract and results; it is a Hedges' g, not a percentage.
- Proposed mechanisms (speculative, not tested): reduced intestinal permeability and inflammation in GRC, improved gut microbiota, and better absorption of iodine, selenium, zinc, vitamin D and levothyroxine.
- Authors' limitations: scanty data, small total n; one GRC study did not biopsy anti-tTG-positive patients, so undiagnosed asymptomatic CD is possible (incidental anti-tTG in adults has highly variable specificity); some patients were on stable LT4.

## Relevance to Cureva

- Suggests any antibody benefit of GFD may be confined to HT patients with gluten-related conditions (NCGS or anti-tTG positivity), which supports screening rather than blanket GFD.
- TSH/FT4 improvement may reflect LT4 or micronutrient absorption rather than immunological change.
- Authors flag metabolic risk of processed gluten-free foods if used at scale.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- Pobłocki 2021 describes itself as randomized, but this meta-analysis classified it as a prospective cohort and extracted only the GFD arm (n=28 at 6 months); controlled between-group comparisons were therefore discarded.
- Abbott 2019 tested an autoimmune protocol diet (broader elimination than gluten), so it is not a pure GFD exposure.
- [[rodziewicz-2024-gluten-free-diet-microbiome-autoimmune-thyroiditis]] found no antibody benefit and pro-inflammatory microbiome shifts with GFD in AIT women.
- With k=4 and n=87, the near-significant antibody signals are fragile; the TSH/FT4 findings rest on uncontrolled pre/post data.

## Open Questions

- Do HT patients without any gluten-related condition benefit at all?
- Are TSH/FT4 changes driven by LT4 absorption, and do LT4 doses change?
- Adequately powered randomized trials with objective adherence markers and microbiome endpoints are lacking.
