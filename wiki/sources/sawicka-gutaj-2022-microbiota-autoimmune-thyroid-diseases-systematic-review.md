# Microbiota Alterations in Patients with Autoimmune Thyroid Diseases: A Systematic Review

## Key Findings

- **HT richness higher than controls in pooled analysis**: ACE p=0.014, Chao1 p=0.008, Shannon higher at margin (p=0.068), Simpson not increased; HT diversity pooling draws on only 2 HT studies (Ishaq 2017, Zhao 2018) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Graves' trended toward lower diversity** on all indices, Chao1 borderline (p=0.068) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **HT pooled abundances did not differ from controls**: Bacteroidetes 39.0% vs 33.9% (p=0.628), Bacteroides 19.6% vs 19.5% (p=0.985), Prevotella 15.0% vs 9.5% (p=0.513), Firmicutes 50.0% vs 50.2% (p=0.986), Bifidobacterium 4.4% vs 3.5% (p=0.794) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Graves' showed non-significant trends** toward higher Bacteroidetes (49.0% vs 33.9%, p=0.089) and Bifidobacterium (9.0% vs 3.5%, p=0.274); the abstract's wording of "significant tendencies" is stronger than Table 4 supports (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **TPOAb showed the most correlations with taxa**, but correlation directions were inconsistent across studies (systematic review, no pooling)

## Methods

- **Design**: Systematic review per PRISMA with a meta-analysis component (pooled SMDs for diversity indices; pooled proportions for relative abundance; MedCalc 19.5.3; subgroups GD and HT). PROSPERO CRD42022335984.
- **Search**: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science up to 23 May 2022, studies after 2000, English only.
- **Eligibility (PICOS)**: AITD patients any age, both sexes; outcomes = richness, diversity, abundance; case-control, cohort, cross-sectional designs. Excluded studies without diversity indices, reviews, case reports, letters, conference reports, and patients with other autoimmune diseases.
- **Included**: 16 studies from 5 countries (13 China/Taiwan, 1 Spain, 1 Egypt, 1 Brazil): 761 AITD patients (563 GD, 198 HT) and 488 controls. HT studies: Ishaq 2017 (29 HT), Liu 2020 (45 euthyroid + 18 hypothyroid HT), Zhao 2018 (28 exploration + 22 validation), Cayres 2021 (40), Cornejo-Pareja 2020 (9 HT), El-Zawawy 2021 (7 HT). All used fecal samples with 16S rRNA sequencing (one 16S rDNA).
- **Quality**: NHLBI Study Quality Assessment Tool; 12 studies (75.0%) "good", 4 (25.0%) "intermediate". All studies at Oxford CEBM level 3 or 4.

## Detailed Summary

### Alpha diversity (pooled SMD; refs 30 to 32, 39, 43, 45)
- GD: all indices lower than controls; Chao1 at borderline significance (p=0.068).
- HT: ACE higher (p=0.014), Chao1 higher (p=0.008), Shannon higher at margin (p=0.068); Simpson not increased. Point SMDs and CIs are in forest plots (Figures 3 and 4), not in text.
- Narrative: GD studies mostly reported lower richness (significant in 3 studies) and lower Shannon (significant in 6, including GO); one GD study (Chang 2021) reported non-significantly higher values. For HT, Ishaq 2017 and Zhao 2018 reported higher richness; El-Zawawy and Liu 2020 reported lower Shannon.

### Pooled relative abundance (Table 4; mean %, 95% CI)
| Taxon | GD | HT | Controls | p GD vs C | p HT vs C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bacteroidetes | 48.964 (31.446 to 66.615) | 39.038 (19.009 to 61.224) | 33.948 (26.366 to 41.968) | 0.089 | 0.628 |
| Bacteroides | 24.806 | 19.629 | 19.498 | 0.560 | 0.985 |
| Prevotella | 28.911 | 14.965 | 9.451 | 0.127 | 0.513 |
| Firmicutes | 47.207 | 49.955 | 50.185 | 0.779 | 0.986 |
| Actinobacteria | 4.692 | 3.641 | 2.77 | 0.482 | 0.713 |
| Bifidobacterium | 9.027 | 4.354 | 3.54 | 0.274 | 0.794 |

None of the pooled abundance comparisons reached p<0.05. The discussion nevertheless states Bacteroidetes "was higher in AITD patients"; readers should rely on the table.

### HT-specific per-study alterations (Table 2)
- Ishaq 2017: down Bifidobacterium, Lactobacillus, Dialister.
- Zhao 2018: up Firmicutes, Blautia, Roseburia, Ruminococcus_torques_group, Romboutsia, Dorea, Fusicatenibacter, Eubacterium_hallii_group; down Bacteroidetes, Faecalibacterium, Bacteroides, Prevotella_9, Lachnoclostridium.
- Liu 2020: HTH up Phascolarctobacterium; euthyroid HT up Lachnospiraceae_incertae_sedis, Lactonifactor, Alistipes, Subdoligranulum.
- Cayres 2021: up Bacteroides; down Bifidobacterium.

### Correlations with thyroid parameters (Table 5; narrative)
- TPOAb had the most significant correlations, then TSH and TRAb; few for TGAb.
- Bacteroidetes: negative with TSH, positive with TPOAb and TRAb.
- Veillonella and Streptococcus: negative with TSH, positive with TPOAb and TRAb.
- Faecalibacterium, Phascolarctobacterium, Bacteroides: contrasting directions across studies.

### Authors' stated limitations
Small, sometimes unmatched samples; most studies from Asia; not all studies could be meta-analysed (data only in figures with inaccurate scales); only one study explicitly on GO; oral microbiome not studied. Diet considered in few studies (only five excluded vegetarians); smoking a potential confounder.

## Relevance to Cureva

- No intervention evidence. The null pooled HT abundance differences mean there is no well-supported single-taxon target for HT from this synthesis.
- Notes in passing that methimazole treatment improved diversity in one Graves' study (Chen 2021), relevant to Graves' not HT.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- The abstract states the HT-vs-GD abundance contrast more strongly than the non-significant p-values in Table 4.
- Higher HT richness here conflicts with [[liu-2020-gut-microbiota-hashimotos-euthyroid-hypothyroid]] (included in the review but not in the diversity meta-analysis), which reported lower richness.
- Gong 2021 reported pooled Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus decreases in AITD; here HT Bifidobacterium is not different from controls.

## Open Questions

- HT diversity pooling rests on 2 studies; is the richness increase robust?
- Heterogeneity statistics (I2) for pooled estimates are not reported in text.
- Stratification by thyroid functional state or levothyroxine use was not possible.
