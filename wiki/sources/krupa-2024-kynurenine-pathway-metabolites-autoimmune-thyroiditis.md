# Alteration in kynurenine pathway metabolites in young women with autoimmune thyroiditis

## Key Findings

- **The kynurenine pathway (KP) was shifted in women with AIT without a change in tryptophan**: serum tryptophan 34.49±5.82 (AIT) vs 34.38±5.34 (control); kynurenine (KYN) higher (p<0.01), anthranilic acid (AA) higher (p<0.001), kynurenic acid (KYNA) lower (p<0.05) (case-control)
- **Derived enzyme indices**: KYN/TRP (IDO1/TDO) higher (p<0.05), AA/KYN (kynureninase A) higher (p<0.01), KYNA/KYN (KAT) lower (p<0.05), 3-HAA/AA (anthranilate-3-hydroxylase) lower (p<0.001) (case-control)
- **AA/KYNA ratio was higher in AIT**: median 0.81 (IQR 0.62 to 1.54) vs 0.54 (0.39 to 0.80), p<0.001; the authors propose this ratio as a possibly AIT-specific feature (case-control)
- **KP metabolites tracked peripheral deiodinase activity in AIT**: SPINA-GD correlated with KYNA/KYN (R=0.331, p=0.014), AA/KYN (R=0.287, p=0.034), QA/3-HAA (R=0.311, p=0.033); in stepwise regression FT4, 3-HAA, KYNA, AA and QA together explained 56.5% of SPINA-GD variance (case-control)
- **Discrimination**: AA/KYNA ROC AUC 0.729 (95% CI 0.625 to 0.833); SPINA-GD 0.689 (0.580 to 0.799); AA + AA/KYNA + QA + SPINA-GD AUC 0.836 (0.731 to 0.912), sensitivity 73.9%, specificity 88.9%, derived and tested in the same sample (case-control)

## Methods

- **Design**: Patients vs healthy controls (authors describe it as cross-sectional; classified case-control per repo convention), single centre, recruitment March 2021 to October 2022; a priori power analysis required 30 per group.
- **Population**: Young women aged 19 to 50, residents of Bialystok, Poland. 57 with AIT (clinical exam, elevated TPOAb and/or TgAb, characteristic ultrasound) and 38 age-matched healthy women (normal TSH/FT3/FT4, antibody negative, normal ultrasound). 32 of 57 AIT patients (56%) on levothyroxine (1.14±0.41 µg/kg/day). Excluded: diabetes, other autoimmune or endocrine disease, hepatic or renal failure, other chronic disease, pregnancy/lactation, hormonal contraception, any chronic medication other than levothyroxine.
- **Clinical profile (Table 1)**: age about 32 y in both groups (p=0.8972); BMI 24.80±5.87 (AIT) vs 24.01±5.13 (p=0.5061); hsCRP not different (p=0.7429); TPOAb median 127.00 vs 10.82 IU/mL and TgAb 165.20 vs 16.36 IU/mL (both p<0.0001); TSH not different (2.03 vs 1.96 µIU/mL, p=0.1999); FT3 lower in AIT (4.43 vs 4.75 pmol/L, p=0.0458); FT4 higher in AIT (16.54±2.72 vs 15.22±1.71 pmol/L, p=0.0052); FT3/FT4 0.28±0.06 vs 0.33±0.07 (p=0.0021); SPINA-GD 26.45±5.20 vs 30.42±6.24 nmol/s (p=0.0021).
- **Measurements**: Fasting (≥12 h) morning serum; TRP, KYN, 3-HKYN, KYNA, AA, 3-HAA by HPLC (diode array, electrochemical, fluorescence detection); quinolinic acid (QA) by ELISA (Immusmol). Enzyme activities inferred from product/substrate ratios. SPINA-GD calculated with SPINA Thyr 4.2.
- **Analysis**: Welch t-test or Mann-Whitney U; Pearson correlation (log transformed as needed); stepwise multiple regression on SPINA-GD (FT3 excluded); ROC analysis; Statistica 13.3, MedCalc 22.009.

## Detailed Summary

- Metabolite concentrations other than tryptophan, and most enzyme-ratio values, are presented only in figures with significance stars.
- 3-HKYN, 3-HAA and QA concentrations were reported as comparable to controls, although the text also describes a "slightly increased" QA in AIT.
- **Autoimmunity correlations**: in controls, TPOAb correlated positively with AA and with AA/KYN and inversely with 3-HAA/AA (coefficients in figures only); TgAb vs AA R=0.327, p=0.048. In AIT, TPOAb was associated with QA and QA/3-HAA (figures only).
- **Thyroid-function correlations in AIT**: SPINA-GD positively with KYNA, AA, QA (figures), inversely with 3-HAA/AA and 3-HKYN; SPINA-GD vs FT3 R=0.661 (p<0.0001), vs FT4 R=-0.455 (p=0.004). FT3 vs KYNA/KYN R=0.332 (p=0.013); FT4 vs 3-HAA R=-0.302 (p=0.025); 3-HKYN vs TSH R=-0.351 (p=0.008).
- **In controls**: SPINA-GD vs KYNA R=0.326 (p=0.049), vs KYNA/KYN R=0.458 (p=0.004); FT3 vs AA R=0.340 (p=0.039), vs AA/KYN R=0.360 (p=0.029), vs 3-HAA/AA R=-0.426 (p=0.008).
- Authors interpret AA and QA accumulation as possibly compensatory, supporting deiodinase activity and FT3 generation; this is a hypothesis from correlations (Figure 6 schematic).
- Authors raise vitamin B2 (riboflavin, KMO cofactor) deficiency as an alternative explanation for AA accumulation, citing animal data, but note their unchanged 3-HKYN/KYN ratio does not fit it. Not measured here.
- Background animal findings discussed by the authors (IDO1 in NOD-H2h4 mouse and rat experimental autoimmune thyroiditis) are cited literature, non-clinical (animal model), not generated in this study.
- Authors' stated limitations: cross-sectional design so no causality; relatively small sample; young women only.

## Relevance to Cureva

- The AA/KYNA ratio and KP panel are hypothesis-generating biomarkers only; the combined AUC was not validated in an independent cohort.
- 56% of AIT patients were on levothyroxine, and FT4 was higher in AIT; treatment may influence both KP metabolites and SPINA-GD, so these markers cannot yet be used to monitor interventions.
- Riboflavin status is an untested modifier worth measuring in future KP work in AIT.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- Table 1 places age 32.45±10.78 under controls while the text assigns it to the AIT group (minor reporting inconsistency).
- QA is described both as "slightly increased" and as "comparable" to controls.
- The authors' compensatory interpretation (AA rise boosting deiodinase activity) sits alongside lower SPINA-GD in AIT overall; direction of causality is untested.
- Prior KP findings in other autoimmune endocrinopathies (Graves' disease, type 1 diabetes) are inconsistent, as the authors note.

## Open Questions

- Is the KP shift present in untreated vs levothyroxine-treated patients separately (not stratified)?
- Does gut microbiome composition explain the AA/KYNA imbalance?
- Do the findings replicate in men, older women and independent cohorts, with absolute metabolite concentrations reported?
