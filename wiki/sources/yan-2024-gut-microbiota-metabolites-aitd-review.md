# Unveiling the Role of Gut Microbiota and Metabolites in Autoimmune Thyroid Diseases: Emerging Perspectives

## Key Findings

- **Central thesis**: gut dysbiosis lowers short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) production, which weakens HDAC inhibition and GPCR (GPR41, GPR43, GPR109A) signaling, impairs extrathymic Treg differentiation and shifts the Th17/Treg ratio toward Th17, contributing to AITD; thyroid hormone imbalance in turn reduces microbial diversity (bidirectional axis) (expert-opinion)
- **Human HT findings cited are observational associations**: Firmicutes remains the dominant phylum but with reduced diversity and abundance; lactic acid bacteria and Bifidobacteria decrease; Bacteroides fragilis increases (expert-opinion summarizing case-control studies)
- **Mechanistic support for SCFA/Treg effects comes mostly from non-thyroid animal models** (colitis, EAE, lupus, arthritis), labeled here as non-clinical (animal model) (expert-opinion)
- **Thyroid-specific animal microbiome data cited are from Graves' disease (TSHR-immunized) mouse models**, not HT models (non-clinical, animal model)
- **Trace elements framed as microbiota-dependent**: iodine, selenium, iron and zinc absorption/utilization and vitamin D are presented as linked to AITD severity; low serum iron/selenium are interpreted as deficiency (expert-opinion)

## Methods

- **Design**: Narrative review (no stated search strategy, inclusion criteria, quality assessment or pooling). Evidence level expert-opinion per repo convention; manifest guess "review" is consistent.
- **Scope**: clinical and immune features of AITD; gut microbiota characteristics in GD and HT (Tables 1 and 2); animal models; SCFA and Th17/Treg; microbiota-thyroid hormone correlations; trace elements and vitamin D.
- **Authors and setting**: Zunyi Medical University, Guizhou, China. Funded by National Natural Science Foundation of China (82160154, 81670844) and Guizhou/Zunyi talent programs. No conflicts declared.

## Detailed Summary

### A. Human studies (clinical evidence as cited by the review)

All are observational unless noted; primary citation numbers refer to the review's reference list.

- **HT gut composition**: Firmicutes the most abundant phylum in HT [34; note this is Liu et al. 2023, a Mendelian randomization study, not an observational cohort], but overall diversity and abundance reduced vs. healthy controls [49 Liu et al. 2022 BMC Microbiol; 50 Dong et al., a salivary microbiome/TSH study]. Lactic acid bacteria and Bifidobacteria decrease [52 Zhao et al. 2018 Thyroid]; Bacteroides fragilis increases [53, 54; see citation concerns below].
- **Discriminating taxa**: Bacteroides, Prevotella and Alistipes distinguish AITD patients from healthy individuals [35 Deng et al. 2023, a prospective GD study]. Prevotella 9 more abundant in GD, Roseburia more prevalent in HT, no phylum-level difference between GD and HT [42 Zhao et al. 2022 Pol J Microbiol]. Collinsella and Roseburia described as key AITD markers and Butyricimonas in GD and HT (no citation given).
- **GD diversity**: alpha diversity typically lower in GD [38]; beta diversity separates GD from controls with overlap [39].
- **MR evidence for GD**: Deltaproteobacteria, Mollicutes, Ruminococcus torques group, Oxalobacter and Ruminococcaceae UCG 011 linked to GD [41 Cao et al. 2023, bidirectional two-sample MR].
- **Immune correlations**: Bacteroides positively correlated with CD4+CD25+ Treg, Th2 and serum IL-10, negatively with Th1, Th1/Th2, TNF-alpha and IFN-gamma; Prevotella positively correlated with Th1, Th1/Th2 and TNF-alpha [73 Wu et al. 2023, hypothyroidism in the first half of pregnancy, not HT-specific]. Yersinia enterocolitica, segmented filamentous bacteria and Eubacterium rectale positively, and Bifidobacteria, Lactobacilli and Prevotella negatively, correlated with Th17/Treg ratio [35].
- **SCFA levels**: GC-MS studies reportedly show significantly lower propionate and butyrate and higher isovalerate in AITD; propionate negatively correlates with FT3, FT4 and TRAb and positively with TSH (no citation given for this paragraph).
- **Microbiota vs. thyroid markers**: Ruminococcus and Clostridium positively, and Streptococcus and Veillonella negatively, correlated with TSH [91 Zhou et al. 2014, hyperthyroid vs. healthy]; Synergistetes negatively correlated with TSAbs [17 Cornejo-Pareja et al. 2020 pilot]; Lactobacillus, Ruminococcus, Bifidobacterium and Veillonella positively correlated with TRAbs [43 Yang et al., GD]; Prevotella 9, Actinomyces odontolyticus, Selenomonadales, Coriobacteriia, Collinsella and Negativicutes positively correlated with TPOAbs [9 Chang et al. 2021, GD]; Romboutsia, Lachnoclostridium, Monoglobus, Haemophilus and Streptococcus related to FT3, and Clostridia UCG 014, Faecalibacterium, Lachnospiraceae NK4A136, Eubacterium ruminantium group and Lachnospiraceae UCG 001 negatively correlated with FT4 [49 Liu et al. 2022, HT]; Dorea, Faecalibacterium and Coprococcus negatively correlated with TPOAbs [17].
- **Trace elements and vitamin D (human)**: HT patients often have low selenium [100 Huang et al. 2023, a two-sample MR study]; selenium supplementation reduces antithyroid antibodies (no citation); autoantibody-positive individuals have lower hemoglobin, ferritin and iron, with TPOAb negatively correlated with ferritin and iron [105 Wang et al. 2022, iron status in pregnancy]; zinc lower in hypothyroidism [108 Rabbani et al. 2021, an RCT of zinc, vitamin A and magnesium co-supplementation in hypothyroidism]; AITD patients have more severe vitamin D deficiency [111 Chen et al. 2022, postpartum hypothyroid women].
- **Human interventions mentioned in passing (via table citations)**: probiotic Bifidobacterium longum or prebiotic berberine with methimazole in GD [47, 16]; these are GD, not HT.

### B. Non-clinical (animal model) evidence cited

- **Non-thyroid autoimmunity models**: in IBD mice, butyrate promotes mucosal IL-10 and inhibits IL-17 and IL-22 [55]; in colitis mice, Faecalibacterium prausnitzii and Clostridium butyricum increase SCFAs and Tregs and decrease Th17 [53 Zhou et al., TNBS colitis]; Prevotella alleviates arthritis severity via butyrate [54 Balakrishnan et al., Prevotella histicola]; in EAE, long-chain fatty acids increase Th1/Th17 while SCFAs promote Treg via JNK1 and p38 inhibition [56 Haghikia et al.]; in SLE mice, dietary butyrate and propionate improve skin and kidney lesions via HDAC inhibition [57]; acetate-modified high-amylose maize resistant starch reduces autoreactive T cells [58; citation appears mismatched, see below]; butyrate gavage reduces colonic NF-kB activation [89; cited paper is a CKD renal fibrosis study].
- **Graves' disease mouse models (TSHR immunization, Ad-TSHR289 in female BALB/c)**: reduced CD4+CD25+FoxP3+ Treg proportion [64 Yuan et al. 2016]; decreased alpha diversity and Firmicutes, decreased Akkermansia and Lachnospiraceae NK4A136, increased Faecalibacterium, Ruminococcaceae, Lactobacillus and Odoribacter [65 Liu et al. 2023, pyrroloquinoline quinone study]; mice receiving fecal microbiota from GD patients had higher model success and higher total T4, TRAbs and IL-17A with lower IL-10 after TSHR immunization [66 Su et al. 2020 JCEM; human donors, mouse recipients]; selenium supplementation in a GD mouse model increased diversity [67; cited source is a selenium/gut microflora review]; vitamin D deficient diet increased susceptibility to sustained hyperthyroidism in TSHR-Ab-injected BALB/cJ mice [68 Misharin et al.]; NOD1 activation in high-fat diet mice raised free T4 [69].
- **Thyroid-adjacent mouse data**: Limibacter negatively correlated with TSAbs [36 Moshkelgosha et al. 2021, Graves' orbitopathy mouse model; presented in the review alongside human correlations]; excess iodine reduced circulating thyroid hormones and TSH while increasing FT3 and FT4 in mice [63 Shen et al. 2019, obese vs. normal mice; as stated, internally contradictory]; selenium deficiency triggered intestinal immune response and barrier dysfunction with thyroid dysfunction [103 Lossow et al. 2021]; hypoferremia increased splenic and lymph node Th17 in mice (no direct citation).
- **No HT-specific (e.g. experimental autoimmune thyroiditis) microbiome experiment is described.**

### C. Non-clinical (in vitro) evidence cited

- Butyrate blocks pro-inflammatory Th1 cells in the thyroid via PPAR-gamma signaling [88]; the cited paper (Antonelli et al.) concerns PPAR-alpha agonists modulating CXCL9/CXCL11 in Graves' ophthalmopathy fibroblasts and preadipocytes, so the claim is not directly supported by its citation.
- SCFAs increase FoxP3 expression through histone acetylation, aiding extrathymic Treg differentiation [86], and stimulate IL-10 while reducing CXCL11 and CCL3 [87].

### Citation-accuracy concerns (for the editor)

Several claims cite papers that do not appear to address them: B. fragilis increase in HT cited to a TNBS colitis paper [53] and a Prevotella histicola arthritis paper [54]; Bacteroides fragilis YCH46 raising Treg cited to Su et al. [66], a GD propionate study; acetate starch and autoreactive T cells cited to an E. coli Nissle PCOS paper [58]; butyrate-PPAR claim cited to a PPAR-alpha orbitopathy paper [88]; microbiota-thyroid marker links cited to an NHANES physical activity paper [90]; iodine supplementation reducing F. prausnitzii, Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium cited to a papillary thyroid carcinoma radioiodine study [98]; excess iodine raising Th17 infiltrates cited to a fortified-salt trial [96]; Bacteroides converting selenite to selenocysteine cited to a zinc-selenium supplementation trial [104]. Do not propagate these claims on the strength of this review alone; go to primary sources.

### Author-stated limitations and future directions

Few reports on microbiota at different disease stages; reliance on fecal samples without accounting for genetics, environment, diet and trace element status; small, single-center, short-term studies; effects of probiotics, prebiotics, diet, antibiotics and trace elements not fully understood; calls for multi-center, large-sample cohorts and translational trials.

## Relevance to Cureva

- Frames SCFA restoration and trace-element correction as candidate adjunct strategies, but the review offers no HT-specific intervention data; supporting mechanistic evidence is non-clinical and largely from other autoimmune models or GD.
- Primitive 2 tension: the review reads low serum iron and selenium in AITD as deficiency to be corrected; under the nutritional-immunity frame, low serum iron in an inflamed host may be partly defensive. Flag before translating to supplementation advice.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- Human HT data summarized here (reduced Bifidobacterium/Lactobacillus, raised B. fragilis) contrast with large MR analyses (e.g. [[zhang-2024-gut-microbiota-metabolites-aitd-mendelian-randomization]]) in which no genus showed a robust causal effect on HT, and in which Lactobacillus showed no association.
- Prevotella is described as both decreased in AITD (protective, butyrate) and positively correlated with Th1 and TNF-alpha, an unresolved tension within the review.

## Open Questions

- Is there any HT-model (EAT or NOD.H-2h4) evidence that microbiota manipulation alters thyroiditis, as opposed to GD models?
- Are reduced fecal propionate and butyrate in AITD reproducible, and are they HT-specific?
- Which of the review's trace-element claims hold when traced to primary HT studies rather than pregnancy, hypothyroidism or MR proxies?
