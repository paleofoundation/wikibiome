# The relationship between elevated plasma zonulin levels and Hashimoto's thyroiditis

## Key Findings

- **Plasma zonulin was higher in newly diagnosed HT patients than in age-, gender- and BMI-matched healthy controls**: 7.7±1.5 vs 6.4±1.9 ng/mL, p≤0.001 (case-control)
- **Zonulin remained associated with HT in multivariate logistic regression**: univariate OR 1.58 (95% CI 1.26 to 1.98, p≤0.001); multivariate OR 1.55 (95% CI 1.21 to 2.00, p≤0.001) (case-control)
- **Discriminative power was modest**: ROC AUC 0.705 (95% CI 0.617 to 0.792) (case-control)
- Other variables associated with HT in the models: anti-TPO, AST, TSH (multivariate OR 1.62, 95% CI 1.25 to 2.11), FT3 (multivariate OR 0.35, 95% CI 0.15 to 0.81); urea in univariate only (case-control)
- The cohort was almost entirely female (75/77 HT, 65/66 controls), Turkish adults in Istanbul (case-control)

## Methods

- **Design**: Case-control (patients vs healthy controls), single centre
- **Population**: 77 newly diagnosed HT patients (75 F, 2 M; mean age 36.8±9.3 y) and 66 age-, gender- and BMI-matched healthy subjects (65 F, 1 M; 34.6±11.4 y) from the Internal Medicine outpatient clinic of Kanuni Sultan Süleyman Training and Research Hospital, Istanbul, Turkey. HT defined by raised TSH, low FT4, elevated anti-TPO, examination and differential diagnosis. Controls had no systemic or autoimmune disease history. BMI 25.9±5.0 (HT) vs 25.0±4.9 (control), p=0.352.
- **Measurements**: Fasting (≥8 h) morning blood; plasma zonulin by ELISA (Bioassay Technology Laboratory, Shanghai, cat no E3704Hu; intra-assay CV <8%, inter-assay <10%); CBC, liver enzymes, lipids, HbA1c, electrolytes, urea, creatinine, uric acid, TSH, FT3, FT4, anti-TPO.
- **Analysis**: Student's t-test or Mann-Whitney U; chi-square or Fisher; univariate and multivariate logistic regression; ROC analysis; SPSS 22. No a priori power analysis.

## Detailed Summary

- Significantly different between HT and controls (Table 2): urea 27.8±24.3 vs 22.9±7.2 mg/dL (p=0.010); AST 18.0±7.8 vs 15.5±2.6 U/L (p=0.005); TSH 4.9±11.3 vs 2.0±1.3 uIU/mL (p≤0.001); FT3 3.0±0.5 vs 3.2±0.5 pg/mL (p=0.008, lower in HT); anti-TPO 259.5±179.0 vs 11.9±5.0 IU/mL (p≤0.001); zonulin 7.7±1.5 vs 6.4±1.9 ng/mL (p≤0.001).
- Not different: glucose, lipids, uric acid, creatinine, HbA1c, ALT, GGT, Na, K, FT4 (1.2±0.5 vs 1.3±0.7, p=0.935), WBC, platelets, MPV, Hb.
- Logistic regression (Table 3): urea univariate OR 1.05 (1.01 to 1.10, p=0.047); anti-TPO univariate OR 1.49 (1.06 to 2.08, p=0.022), multivariate OR 1.21 (1.07 to 1.36, p=0.002); AST univariate OR 1.16 (1.04 to 1.29, p=0.007), multivariate OR 1.21 (1.07 to 1.36, p=0.002); TSH univariate OR 1.62 (1.29 to 2.04), multivariate 1.62 (1.25 to 2.11); FT3 univariate OR 0.42 (0.21 to 0.86, p=0.017), multivariate 0.35 (0.15 to 0.81, p=0.015); zonulin univariate OR 1.58 (1.26 to 1.98), multivariate 1.55 (1.21 to 2.00).
- ROC: AUC 0.705 (0.617 to 0.792). Text reports a cut-off ≥4 ng/mL with sensitivity 100% and specificity 90.6% (Table 4 lists 90.9%).
- Authors' stated limitations: small sample, no power analysis, no other gut permeability markers, nutritional status not assessed. No microbiome data collected despite the microbiota framing.
- No external funding; no conflicts declared.

## Relevance to Cureva

- Supports gut barrier integrity as a plausible target to investigate in HT, but provides no intervention data.
- The absolute between-group difference is small (1.3 ng/mL) with overlapping distributions; zonulin is not usable as a stand-alone diagnostic marker on this evidence.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- **Internal inconsistencies in the ROC reporting**: Table 4 lists 100% sensitivity and 100% specificity at a 1.0 ng/mL cut-off and specificity that falls as the cut-off rises, which is incompatible with the reported AUC of 0.705; the text also conflates specificity with NPV and sensitivity with PPV. The cut-off claim should not be cited.
- In Table 3 the multivariate anti-TPO and AST rows carry identical values (OR 1.21, 1.07 to 1.36, p=0.002), which may be a transcription error.
- The paper alternates between "plasma" and "serum" zonulin; methods state blood for zonulin was collected in tubes without additives.
- Absolute zonulin values (about 6 to 8 ng/mL) differ roughly 7 to 9 fold from the pediatric study (43 to 59 ng/mL), reflecting different commercial ELISA kits; values are not comparable across studies.

## Open Questions

- Does zonulin correlate with anti-TPO titre or disease duration in adults (not reported)?
- Is the zonulin signal accompanied by direct permeability or microbiome changes in the same patients?
- Would levothyroxine treatment alter zonulin levels in newly diagnosed HT?
