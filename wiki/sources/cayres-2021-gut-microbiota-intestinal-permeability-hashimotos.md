# Detection of Alterations in the Gut Microbiota and Intestinal Permeability in Patients With Hashimoto Thyroiditis

## Key Findings

- **Bacteroides higher and Bifidobacterium lower in HT stool.** Median Bacteroides 2,344 vs 221.3 REU/200 mg stool (p<0.0001); median Bifidobacterium 70.86 vs 830.6 REU/200 mg stool (p=0.005), HT vs controls, by targeted qPCR (case-control)
- **Serum zonulin elevated in HT.** Mean ± SE 30.92 ± 2.36 ng/ml in HT vs 19.01 ± 2.98 in controls (p=0.002; the control unit is printed as pg/ml, almost certainly a typo); zonulin correlated with IFN-gamma (r=0.52, p=0.042) and inversely with IL-2 (r=-0.51, p=0.042) in HT (case-control)
- **No difference in systemic cytokines** (IL-2, IL-4, IL-6, IL-10, IL-17A, IFN-gamma, TNF; all p>0.05) (case-control)
- **Levothyroxine status tracked with Lactobacillus.** Untreated HT (n=8) median Lactobacillus 47.73 vs LT4-treated (n=28) 0.8065 REU/200 mg stool (p=0.020) (case-control, within-patient subgroup)
- **Diet differed between groups** and animal-derived protein intake correlated inversely with Bacteroides in HT (r=-0.61, p=0.002) (case-control)

## Methods

- **Design**: Observational patients vs healthy controls comparison (case-control by repo convention; manifest guessed "observational").
- **Population**: Brazil (Barretos Cancer Hospital ethics approval 1,359/2017). 40 HT patients aged 23 to 82 (mean 48.9 ± 13.3 y); per Table 1, 36 female and 4 male. 53 healthy controls aged 18 to 79 (mean 45.6 ± 16.7 y); control sex not reported. HT defined by increased TSH and decreased FT4, with or without elevated anti-TPO/anti-Tg. 28 patients on oral levothyroxine, 8 untreated (per Table 1 and Lactobacillus subgroup analysis); many patients have thyroid labs "not determined" in Table 1. Disease duration 0.9 to 42 years.
- **Exclusions (both groups)**: anti-inflammatories, immunosuppressants, antibiotics or vaccination in prior 30 days; GI surgery, IBD, chronic diarrhea.
- **Measurements**: Stool DNA (200 mg, QIAamp Fast DNA Stool Mini Kit); real-time PCR with group-specific 16S primers for Bacteroides, Bifidobacterium, Clostridium coccoides, C. coccoides-Eubacterium rectale, Clostridium leptum, Lactobacillus, Prevotella, Roseburia, normalized to universal primer, expressed as REU per 200 mg stool. Serum cytokines by cytometric bead array (BD Th1/Th2/Th17). Serum zonulin by ELISA (Elabscience). Food frequency questionnaire.
- **Statistics**: Mann-Whitney U for REU; unpaired t-test with Welch correction for cytokines and zonulin (Figure 3 legend says Mann-Whitney); Pearson chi-square for diet; Spearman for correlations; p<0.05. No multiple-comparison correction reported.

## Detailed Summary

### Microbiota (qPCR, HT vs controls)
- Bacteroides: median 2,344 vs 221.3 REU/200 mg (p<0.0001), increased in HT.
- Bifidobacterium: median 70.86 vs 830.6 REU/200 mg (p=0.005), decreased in HT.
- Not significant (p>0.05), HT vs control medians: C. coccoides 43.51 vs 51.21; C. coccoides-E. rectale 48.03 vs 78.04; C. leptum 1.060 vs 1,762; Lactobacillus 0.974 vs 4.933; Prevotella 12.53 vs 0.530; Roseburia 385.8 vs 635.9.

### Clinical correlations (HT only, Spearman)
- C. coccoides vs TSH: r=0.40, p=0.023. C. coccoides-E. rectale vs TSH: r=0.45, p=0.010.
- Roseburia vs FT4: r=-0.49, p=0.015.
- C. coccoides vs disease duration: r=0.30, p=0.040.
- No correlations with anti-TPO or anti-Tg (p>0.05).
- Lactobacillus: LT4 (N=28) median 0.8065 vs untreated (N=8) 47.73 REU/200 mg, p=0.020.

### Diet
- Daily intake, HT vs controls: vegetables 59% vs 53.3%; fruits 38.5% vs 43.3%; animal-derived proteins 51.3% vs 30%; dairy 48.7% vs 60%; saturated fats 17.9% vs 16.7%; carbohydrates 38.5% vs 43.3%. Distribution differences significant for all categories (p<0.001; saturated fat p=0.005). The diet table covers 39 patients and 30 controls, fewer than the enrolled 53 controls.
- Animal protein vs Bacteroides in HT: r=-0.61, p=0.002.

### Cytokines (mean ± SE, HT vs controls, all p>0.05)
IL-2 0.3369 vs 0.3800; IL-4 0.4081 vs 0.3791; IL-6 1.121 vs 1.375; IL-10 0.3192 vs 0.2884; IL-17A 0.5835 vs 0.3981; IFN-gamma 0.4473 vs 0.3438; TNF 1.154 vs 1.317 pg/ml.

### Intestinal permeability
- Serum zonulin 30.92 ± 2.36 ng/ml (HT) vs 19.01 ± 2.98 (controls, unit printed as pg/ml), p=0.002.
- In HT, zonulin vs IFN-gamma r=0.52, p=0.042; vs IL-2 r=-0.51, p=0.042.

### Authors' interpretation
The authors conclude there are alterations in microbiota and intestinal permeability in Brazilian HT patients and suggest diet may modulate the microbiota; they speculate the higher Lactobacillus in untreated patients could relate to SIBO. These are hypotheses, not tested here.

## Relevance to Cureva

- Supports a "barrier plus Bifidobacterium depletion" hypothesis in HT, but only as association; no intervention was tested.
- The Lactobacillus difference by levothyroxine status is a reminder that LT4 treatment is a confounder to stratify on in any HT microbiome protocol.
- Diet differed between groups, so dietary pattern is a confounder for the Bacteroides signal.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- Zhao 2018 (16S sequencing, China) reported Bacteroides **decreased** in HT; this study reports it **increased** by qPCR. The authors attribute discrepancies to methodology, geography, lifestyle and diet.
- Prevotella median was numerically much higher in HT (12.53 vs 0.530) but not significant here, whereas other HT studies report Prevotella decreased.
- [[li-2026-thyroid-functional-state-gut-dysbiosis-hashimotos]] reports Bifidobacterium **increased** in dysthyroid HT, opposite to this study.

## Open Questions

- Targeted qPCR of 8 groups cannot describe community structure or diversity; would 16S/shotgun sequencing reproduce the Bacteroides increase?
- Zonulin ELISA kits (including Elabscience) have known specificity concerns in the wider literature; the paper does not address assay validity. Is zonulin elevation reproducible with other barrier markers (LPS, LBP, lactulose/mannitol)?
- Control sex distribution and BMI are not reported; matching is unclear.
- No authors' limitations section; small untreated subgroup (n=8) drives the Lactobacillus finding.
