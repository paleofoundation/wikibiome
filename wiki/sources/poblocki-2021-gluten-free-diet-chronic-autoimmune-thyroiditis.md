# Whether a Gluten-Free Diet Should Be Recommended in Chronic Autoimmune Thyroiditis or Not? A 12-Month Follow-Up

(Published title contains a dash between "Not?" and "A 12-Month Follow-Up"; rendered here with a space per house style.)

## Key Findings

- **No between-group difference in thyroid antibodies after 12 months.** Median anti-TPO and anti-TG did not differ between the gluten-free diet group (GFDG) and controls (CG) at baseline, 3, 6 or 12 months (12-month anti-TPO 133.40 vs 134.50 IU/mL, p=0.455; anti-TG 93.89 vs 123.60 IU/mL, p=0.515) (randomized-controlled-trial)
- **TSH was lower on GFD at 12 months.** Median TSH 1.44 (95% CI 1.14 to 1.88) vs 1.93 (1.40 to 3.01) uIU/mL, p=0.044; the 12-month change in TSH also differed (p=0.039), as did the change in fT4 (increase in GFDG, p=0.022) (randomized-controlled-trial)
- **Within-group, log-transformed analyses** showed anti-TG reductions in the GFDG at 3, 6 and 12 months (p=0.023, 0.019, 0.049) and an anti-TPO reduction at 3 months only (p=0.010); the control group also showed an anti-TG reduction at 6 months (p=0.016) (randomized-controlled-trial, within-group)
- **No antibody normalisation** occurred in any of the 62 women during follow-up (randomized-controlled-trial)
- **Authors' conclusion:** GFD may be attempted after excluding celiac disease, but there are "no clear indications" for routine GFD in HT; they suggest general diet modification and education, not gluten removal per se, may explain benefits (randomized-controlled-trial)

## Methods

- **Design:** prospective, open-label, two-arm study with random assignment ("randomly assigned", "randomized into two groups using a randomization program") to GFD or usual gluten-containing diet; 12-month follow-up with visits at baseline, 3, 6 and 12 months. Per-protocol analysis of women attending at least 3 appointments.
- **Population:** Caucasian women aged 18 to 55 with chronic autoimmune thyroiditis (ultrasound pattern plus anti-TPO >34 IU/mL and/or anti-TG >115 IU/mL), Szczecin, Poland. All consumed about 10 g gluten/day for at least 3 months before entry. Celiac disease excluded by anti-tTG IgA and total IgA; 2 anti-tTG-positive women underwent duodenal biopsy with no villous atrophy.
- **n:** 92 enrolled (CG n=50, GFDG n=42); attendance 35/35 at 3 months, 32/28 at 6 months, 31/31 at 12 months; final analysis n=62 (31 vs 31). All 62 were on levothyroxine for hypothyroidism or subclinical hypothyroidism and were clinically euthyroid. Mean age 36.86 (SD 8.64) years, BMI 25.31 (SD 4.80).
- **Intervention:** GFD (gluten-free products <=20 mg gluten/kg), dietitian-supervised with food diaries, education on low glycemic index foods, fiber, omega-3 fatty acids. Adherence by questionnaire: excellent 9, good 17, fair 5. Controls continued their usual ("average Pole's") diet without monitoring.
- **Measurements:** TSH, fT3, fT4, anti-TPO, anti-TG by ECLIA (Roche Cobas 6000).
- **Analysis:** Mann-Whitney U for between-group medians; Wilcoxon signed-rank on log-transformed values for within-group changes; p<0.05. No multiplicity correction reported.

## Detailed Summary

**Baseline (Table 1):** groups did not differ (all p>0.05). Median anti-TPO 114.40 (CG) vs 207.30 (GFDG) IU/mL; median anti-TG 231.60 vs 145.80 IU/mL; median TSH 2.31 vs 2.44 uIU/mL.

**Between-group medians (Table 2):**

| Parameter | Time | CG (n=31) | GFDG (n=31) | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TSH (uIU/mL) | 12 mo | 1.93 (1.40 to 3.01) | 1.44 (1.14 to 1.88) | 0.044 |
| fT4 (ng/dL) | 12 mo | 1.32 | 1.42 | 0.177 |
| anti-TPO (IU/mL) | 12 mo | 134.50 | 133.40 | 0.455 |
| anti-TG (IU/mL) | 12 mo | 123.60 | 93.89 | 0.515 |

Only TSH at 12 months differed. Medians of anti-TPO and anti-TG in the GFDG declined at each visit (anti-TPO 207.30, 174.10, 141.85, 133.40; anti-TG 145.80, 131.70, 100.13, 93.89) but not significantly versus controls.

**Change from baseline (Table 3; positive delta = decrease):** TSH change at 12 months 0.33 (CG) vs 1.17 (GFDG), p=0.039; fT4 change -0.01 vs -0.10 (i.e., increase in GFDG), p=0.022. No significant differences for anti-TPO, anti-TG or fT3 at any time.

**Within-group, log-transformed (Table 4):**
- GFDG: anti-TPO p=0.010 (3 mo), 0.581 (6 mo), 0.302 (12 mo); anti-TG p=0.023, 0.019, 0.049; TSH p=0.001, 0.048, <0.001; fT4 p=0.290, 0.007, <0.001.
- CG: anti-TG p=0.016 at 6 months; no other significant changes.

**Dropout reasons (GFDG):** pregnancy 2, difficulty following GFD 3, non-adherence to levothyroxine 2. (CG): pregnancy 6, endometriosis 1. Others gave no reason.

**Authors' stated limitations:** women only; all on levothyroxine (which can itself lower antibodies); adherence not verified by urinary or fecal gluten peptides; control diet not monitored; possible non-celiac gluten sensitivity. Authors also note seasonal fluctuation of anti-TPO and anti-TG as a possible explanation for antibody changes. Discussion also mentions that unsupervised GFD can increase rice-based intake (arsenic, cadmium, lead, copper) and maize mycotoxins, and that Polish diets are often low in iodine and selenium (background discussion, not measured).

## Relevance to Cureva

- Evidence against routine GFD for antibody lowering in non-celiac HT. Possible signal on TSH/fT4 in levothyroxine-treated patients, which the authors interpret as an absorption effect; dose was not reported as changed.
- Diet arm bundled GFD with broad nutrition education, so any effect cannot be attributed to gluten removal alone.
- Practical caution raised by authors: gluten-free processed foods and rice-heavy substitution may raise heavy-metal and mycotoxin exposure.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- **Design classification differs across sources.** The paper describes random assignment, but [[piticchio-2023-gluten-free-diet-autoimmune-thyroiditis-meta-analysis]] classified it as a prospective cohort and used n=28 at 6 months. Unequal initial allocation (50 vs 42), open-label design, about 33% attrition and per-protocol analysis weaken the randomized label.
- **Internal inconsistency:** the Discussion states the control group showed an anti-TPO decrease after 6 months, whereas Table 4 shows the control 6-month change was in anti-TG (p=0.016), not anti-TPO (p=0.981).
- Krysiak et al. (cited) reported anti-TPO and anti-TG reductions with 6 months of GFD in untreated women with HT; this study did not replicate a between-group antibody effect.

## Open Questions

- Does the TSH/fT4 shift reflect levothyroxine absorption, and would it persist with dose-controlled designs?
- Would objective adherence markers (gluten immunogenic peptides) change the result?
- Are any effects driven by the education component (fiber, omega-3) rather than gluten removal?
- Effect in men, in untreated euthyroid HT, and in non-celiac gluten sensitivity subgroups.
