# The changes in peripheral blood Th17 and Treg ratios in Hashimoto's thyroiditis are accompanied by differential PD-1/PD-L1 expression

## Key Findings

- **Peripheral Th17 cells (CD4+CD3+IL-17+IFN-γ-) were higher in newly diagnosed HT** than normal controls: 6.38±1.32% vs 3.12±0.66% of CD4+ T cells, t=14.110, p<0.001 (case-control)
- **Tregs (CD4+CD25+FOXP3+) were lower in HT**: 3.82±1.48% vs 5.61±1.60%, t=-4.599, p<0.001 (case-control)
- **Fewer Th17 cells expressed the inhibitory checkpoint PD-1 in HT**: 6.46±2.77% vs 18.51±3.96%, t=-14.842, p<0.001; PD-L1+ Th17 did not differ (p>0.05) (case-control)
- **More Tregs expressed PD-1 and PD-L1 in HT**: PD-1+ Treg 17.01±3.04% vs 10.23±2.77% (t=8.850, p<0.001); PD-L1+ Treg 16.60±9.58% vs 11.36±10.14% (t=2.089; p<0.05 in Table 2, p<0.005 in abstract) (case-control)
- Authors propose loss of PD-1-mediated restraint on Th17 cells as a possible pathogenic mechanism; the meaning of higher PD-1/PD-L1 on Tregs is explicitly left unresolved (case-control)

## Methods

- **Design**: Case-control, single centre (endocrinology outpatient, First Affiliated Hospital of Bengbu Medical College, China); a priori sample size calculation (α=0.05, power 0.90, PASS 11) requiring at least 21 controls and 43 HT.
- **Population**: 53 initially diagnosed HT (1 male, 52 female; age 38.91±13.02 y) and 21 age- and sex-matched controls from physical examination (2 male, 19 female; 38.57±14.52 y); overall age range 15 to 66 y. HT per Chinese guidelines (diffuse goiter on palpation/ultrasound, TPOAb and/or TgAb positive, normal thyroid function or raised TSH; FNA cytology optional). Excluded: other autoimmune or inflammatory disease, acute or chronic infection, recent thyroid hormone, glucocorticoids or immunosuppressants, malignancy, immunodeficiency, pregnancy or lactation.
- **Clinical labs**: FT3 4.50±0.94 vs 4.73±0.44 pmol/L (p=0.279); FT4 15.07±4.69 vs 15.60±2.19 (p=0.515); TSH 8.06±17.87 vs 2.29±1.12 mIU/L (p=0.024); TPOAb 711.0±576.51 vs 56.59±9.75 U/mL (p<0.001); TgAb 340.2±223.02 vs 33.04±13.84 U/mL (p<0.001).
- **Measurements**: Whole-blood flow cytometry; Th17 after 4 h ex vivo stimulation (Cell Stimulation Cocktail), staining for CD3, CD4, IL-17A, IFN-γ, PD-1 (CD279), PD-L1 (CD274); Treg staining for CD4, CD25, FOXP3, PD-1, PD-L1; isotype and compensation controls.
- **Analysis**: SPSS 26; t-test or non-parametric tests; chi-square. No correlation results with thyroid parameters are reported despite correlation methods being listed.

## Relevance to Cureva

- PD-1/PD-L1 is relevant mainly as a safety signal (checkpoint-inhibitor thyroiditis is mentioned by the authors) and a speculative therapeutic direction; no intervention data.
- Th17 and Treg percentages (with PD-1 status) are candidate immune readouts for HT intervention trials.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- PD-L1+ Treg significance is reported as p<0.005 in the abstract but p<0.05 in Table 2; with t=2.089 and these group sizes, p<0.05 is the consistent value.
- Table 1 gives HT gender as "1/53" while the text states 1 male and 52 female.
- The preliminary PD-1+ Th17 control mean used for the power calculation (13.41%) differs from the final control mean (18.51%).
- Higher PD-1/PD-L1 on Tregs conflicts with literature that PD-1/PD-L1 promotes Treg induction; authors acknowledge this unresolved contradiction.
- Ethics statement says consent was given by "legal guardian/next of kin", an apparent template statement for an adult outpatient cohort.

## Open Questions

- Do PD-1+ Th17 frequencies correlate with TPOAb, TgAb or TSH (not reported)?
- Are the changes present in thyroid-infiltrating cells, not only blood?
- Do Th17/Treg and PD-1 profiles shift with treatment or with gut-directed interventions?
