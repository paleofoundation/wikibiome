# Selenium Supplementation in Patients with Hashimoto Thyroiditis: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Clinical Trials

## Key Findings

- **TSH decreased with selenium only in patients without thyroid hormone replacement therapy (THRT)**: SMD -0.21 (95% CI -0.41 to -0.02), 7 cohorts, 869 participants, I2 = 0%. In all patients TSH did not change significantly: SMD -0.21 (95% CI -0.43 to 0.01), 26 cohorts, 2063 participants, I2 = 59% (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **TPOAb decreased**: SMD -0.96 (95% CI -1.36 to -0.56), 29 cohorts, 2358 participants, I2 = 90%; GRADE low; rank correlation test indicated funnel asymmetry (p = 0.04) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Malondialdehyde (MDA, oxidative stress marker) decreased**: SMD -1.16 (95% CI -2.29 to -0.03), 3 cohorts, I2 = 85% (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **No significant effect** on fT4 (SMD 0.05), fT3 (SMD 0.51), T4, T3, TGAb (SMD -0.27, 95% CI -0.59 to 0.06), thyroid volume, IL-2, or IL-10 (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Safety**: adverse events comparable between selenium and control (OR 0.89, 95% CI 0.46 to 1.75; 16 cohorts; 1339 participants; I2 = 0%; GRADE moderate) at doses of 80 to 400 ug/day for up to 12 months (systematic-review-meta-analysis)

## Methods

- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis of parallel RCTs, PRISMA 2020, Cochrane Handbook; PROSPERO CRD42022308377.
- **Search**: MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL (printed "CINHAL"), Web of Science, Google Scholar (first 200 results), Cochrane CENTRAL; forward and backward citation searching. Methods state inception to 5 April 2022 with an update on 14 November 2023; the abstract states inception to January 2023.
- **Eligibility**: RCTs of selenium supplementation in HT patients (euthyroid, subclinical, or overt hypothyroid; with or without THRT); no restrictions on language, date, age, duration, dose, or compound.
- **Included**: 1025 records, 687 after deduplication, 60 full-text reports; 35 unique studies (42 cohorts) in the systematic review, 32 in the meta-analysis. Published 2002 to 2021; 31 to 364 participants per study; Europe 19, Middle East 4, Asia 11, South America 1. Two studies in children and adolescents (18 years or younger) and three in pregnant women. 32 of 35 studies (91%) had female preponderance.
- **Interventions**: selenomethionine, sodium selenite, selenious acid, or selenium yeast; mostly 200 ug/day (range 60 to 400 ug/day); 2 to 12 months (mean maximum 5.8 months). Comparators: placebo or no treatment (several open-label trials).
- **Selenium status**: 18 studies measured baseline selenium: 9 severely deficient, 7 mildly deficient, 2 sufficient.
- **Primary outcome**: TSH in patients without THRT. Secondary: fT4, T4, fT3, T3, TPOAb, TGAb, TRAb, ultrasound, immune markers, patient-reported outcomes, adverse events.
- **Analysis**: random-effects SMD (continuous) or OR (dichotomous) using R metafor; subgroup analyses and meta-regression (dose, duration, thyroid status, sex, age group, THRT, selenium status, blinding); leave-one-out and Baujat plots; Egger's and rank correlation tests; RoB 2; GRADE.

### Subgroups and sensitivity

- **TSH**: in THRT-treated patients only, SMD -0.24 (95% CI -0.65 to 0.16; 12 cohorts; 794; I2 64%). The without-THRT result held or strengthened when restricted to euthyroid or subclinical patients, adults, and selenium for under 6 months; selenium compound significantly affected results (p = 0.03). Esposito 2017 was excluded for missing and conflicting data.
- **fT4**: restricting to adults showed an increase (SMD 0.19, 95% CI 0.01 to 0.38; 17 cohorts; 1152; I2 0%); age group (p < 0.01) and thyroid status (p = 0.03) influenced results.
- **TPOAb**: 10 of 31 cohorts individually showed a significant decrease; 21 did not. Pooled effect similar or stronger in overt hypothyroidism, adults, THRT-treated patients, doses above 100 ug/day, selenomethionine, and blinded trials; thyroid status influenced results (p = 0.03). Heterogeneity fell when restricted to severely selenium-deficient and female-only cohorts. In pregnant women, TPOAb fell at delivery and 5 to 6 months postpartum but not during gestation.
- **TGAb**: 4 of 19 cohorts decreased, 14 no change, 1 increased; decrease seen in patients without THRT (subgroup).
- **TRAb**: one study (Zhang 2020) reported a decrease.
- **Echogenicity**: improved in 2 of 8 cohorts (25% and 50% of selenium patients vs 5% and 12% of controls); no effect in 6.
- **Immune and antioxidant markers**: GPX increased in 4 cohorts, unchanged in 3; CXCL-9/-10/-11 decreased in 2 cohorts, no effect in 1; IFN-gamma and TNF-alpha inconsistent; CD4/CD8 ratio increased in 2 cohorts.
- **Quality of life**: 2 of 6 blinded cohorts reported improved well-being; 4 found no difference.
- **Adverse events**: 13 of 18 cohorts reported none; 5 reported 2 to 19 events (nausea, vomiting, fever, dizziness, chest tightness, bloating, GI problems, hair loss, miscarriages). No study assessed severity or causality per ICH E2A.

### Risk of bias

Of 31 studies reporting TSH, 12 (39%) had some concerns and 19 (61%) high risk of bias, mostly from randomization process and selection of reported results. Only 6 (19%) were prospectively registered; 8 (26%) described randomization. All studies raised at least some concerns. 16 studies (52%) measured serum selenium and found an increase to around 100 ug/L in treated participants (one study found no significant increase).

### Author-stated limitations

- Different TPOAb and TGAb assays (addressed with SMD, so only effect size, not clinical magnitude, is interpretable).
- Serum selenium measurement not standardized.
- Dietary habits, baseline iodine and iron status, and compliance rarely reported.
- THRT dose and timing not considered; no washout or correction before laboratory measurement.
- Underrepresentation of men; no sex-stratified analyses.
- All studies had at least some concerns on RoB 2.

### Safety context in the discussion

Acute toxicity typically at 300 to 400 ug/day; EFSA tolerable upper intake 255 ug/day; one trial raised type 2 diabetes concerns with 200 ug/day over 7.7 years in a non-deficient population; supplementation is not recommended when serum or plasma selenium is at least 122 ug/L.

## Relevance to Cureva

- Intervention hypothesis: selenium (often 200 ug/day selenomethionine) may lower TPOAb and modestly lower TSH in HT patients not yet on levothyroxine; the TSH effect is small (SMD -0.21).
- STOP signals: avoid supplementation if serum or plasma selenium is 122 ug/L or higher; respect EFSA upper limit of 255 ug/day; toxicity reported at 300 to 400 ug/day.
- Evidence certainty is moderate overall, low for TPOAb; most trials at high risk of bias.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- The TPOAb pooled effect is significant, yet 21 of 31 cohorts individually found no effect and the rank test suggested funnel asymmetry (p = 0.04).
- Hu 2021, which itself reported significant between-group reductions in TPOAb and TGAb change scores, is coded here as no significant effect on TPOAb and TGAb (likely because 6-month absolute values did not differ between groups).
- Numeric inconsistencies in the paper: TSH without THRT CI is -0.43 to -0.02 in the abstract but -0.41 to -0.02 in Table 2 and text; MDA is 248 participants in abstract and Table 2 but 148 in the text, with upper CI -0.02 (abstract) vs -0.03 (table); search end date differs between abstract (January 2023) and methods (14 November 2023 update).
- Earlier meta-analyses found no TSH effect; this one finds an effect only in the non-THRT subgroup.

## Open Questions

- Does selenium delay progression from euthyroid or subclinical HT to overt hypothyroidism?
- Is the benefit confined to selenium-deficient populations (data on sufficient cohorts were scarce)?
- What is the clinical meaning of a TPOAb SMD reduction measured across different assays?
- Do selenoprotein gene polymorphisms or sex modify response?
- Does selenium status interact with gut microbial selenium utilization in HT?
