# Alterations in gut microbiota and fecal metabolites in euthyroid autoimmune thyroiditis during early pregnancy

## Key Findings

- **Diversity unchanged, a few taxa shifted.** No significant differences in alpha diversity (observed species, Chao1, Shannon, Simpson, PD whole tree; p=0.55 to 0.82) or beta diversity; LEfSe (P<0.05, LDA>2) showed o_Rhodospirillales, f_Rhodospirillaceae, g_Peptococcus, f_Peptococcaceae 1 and f_Clostridiales Incertae Sedis XI higher in AIT, and g_Coprobacter higher in controls (case-control)
- **Fecal lipid mediators and bile acids lower in AIT.** KEGG enrichment: arachidonic acid metabolism and serotonergic synapse (positive mode p=0.026; negative mode p=0.0036), alpha-linolenic acid metabolism (p=0.025), bile secretion (p=0.036). Down in AIT: arachidonic acid, thromboxane B2, prostaglandins B2, F2alpha and D2, traumatic acid, 13(S)-HOTrE, deoxycholic acid, chenodeoxycholic acid; ouabain up (case-control)
- **Metabolites tracked antibody levels more than microbes did.** Sulfoacetic acid correlated with TPOAb (r=0.623) and TgAb (r=0.548), both P<0.001; arachidonic acid (r=-0.395, P=0.003), prostaglandin B2 (r=-0.494, P<0.001) and deoxycholic acid (r=-0.470, P<0.001) correlated inversely with TPOAb. Only f_Rhodospirillaceae among taxa correlated with TPOAb (r=0.342, P=0.010) (case-control)
- **Lower birth weight in AIT offspring:** 3240.38 ± 464.18 g vs 3584.33 ± 448.23 g, P=0.007; anemia before delivery 3/23 vs 0/30 (P=0.094); no differences in preterm delivery, PROM, postpartum hemorrhage, fetal distress (case-control)
- **Exploratory:** no multiple-comparison correction was applied to metabolite and correlation analyses (authors' statement) (case-control)

## Methods

- **Design:** case-control comparison within the Northeast Regional Maternal-Fetal-Child-Adolescent Cohort; recruitment September 2018 to December 2019 at the First Hospital of China Medical University obstetrics clinic, Shenyang, China. Pregnancy outcomes subsequently recorded.
- **Population:** women aged 20 to 35, long-term residents of Northeast China, 7 to 13 weeks' gestation, no prior thyroid treatment. AIT group (n=26): euthyroid with TPOAb >34 IU/mL and/or TgAb >115 IU/mL. Controls (n=30): euthyroid, antibody-negative, matched on age and BMI. Euthyroid defined by first-trimester reference ranges (FT4 13.35 to 19.01 pmol/L; TSH 0.35 to 4.13 mIU/L). Excluded: thyroid dysfunction, pre-pregnancy BMI outside 18.0 to 28.0, smoking, alcohol, GI or chronic inflammatory disease.
- **Power:** a priori 26 per group for Cohen's d=0.8; MetSizeR estimate 17 per group for metabolomics.
- **Measurements:** 16S rDNA V3 to V4 (341F/806R), Illumina MiSeq PE250, OTUs at 97% (USEARCH), QIIME 1.9.1; analysis restricted to genus/family level. Untargeted fecal metabolomics by UHPLC-MS/MS (positive and negative ion modes); differential metabolites by VIP>1.0, FC>1.5 or <0.667, P<0.05. Serum thyroid function and antibodies by Roche E601.
- **Statistics:** t-test or Mann-Whitney U, Fisher's exact test, Spearman correlation; multivariable linear regression adjusting for f_Rhodospirillaceae, age and pre-pregnancy BMI; SPSS 27.0.

## Detailed Summary

**Participants (Table 1):** no differences in BMI (21.67 vs 21.22), age (29.48 vs 30.21 years), FT3, FT4, TSH (median 1.89 vs 1.57 mIU/L, p=0.175), glucose or lipids. TPOAb median 230.65 vs 12.54 IU/mL and TgAb 372.10 vs 10.76 IU/mL (both p<0.01).

**Sequencing:** 56 samples, 3,372,065 high-quality sequences (mean 60,215/sample), 614 OTUs; 505 shared, 68 unique to controls, 41 unique to AIT. Firmicutes and Bacteroidetes >90% of reads; Bacteroides, Prevotella, Faecalibacterium and Roseburia >60% of genus-level reads in both groups.

**Metabolomics:** 706 metabolites (positive mode) and 355 (negative mode). Differential: 89 in positive mode (20 up, 69 down in AIT) and 46 in negative mode (6 up, 40 down).

**Microbe-metabolite correlations (Spearman):** g_Coprobacter inversely with cadaverine (positive mode) and with caprylic acid and 3-amino-1H-pyrazolo[4,3-c]pyridine-4,6-diol (negative mode); g_Peptococcus positively with sulfoacetic acid and a diarylheptanoid, and inversely with 7-methylxanthine and 3-hydroxypicolinic acid.

**Correlations with thyroid parameters (Table 4, whole cohort):**
- f_Rhodospirillaceae: FT4 r=0.344 (P=0.009), TPOAb r=0.342 (P=0.010), TgAb r=0.329 (P=0.013). No other taxon correlated significantly (Bacteroides, Bifidobacterium, Coprobacter, Prevotella, Lactobacillus, Fusicatenibacter, Roseburia, Peptococcus, Faecalibacterium all P>0.05).
- Positive with antibodies: sulfoacetic acid (TPOAb 0.623, TgAb 0.548), caprylic acid (TPOAb 0.314, P=0.018; TgAb 0.400, P=0.002), cadaverine (TgAb 0.278, P=0.038), 3-amino-pyrazolopyridine-diol (TPOAb 0.403, P=0.002).
- Inverse with antibodies: arachidonic acid (TPOAb -0.395, TgAb -0.398), 7-methylxanthine (TPOAb -0.406, P=0.002; TgAb -0.394, P=0.003), prostaglandin F2alpha (TPOAb -0.416, P=0.001; TgAb -0.293, P=0.028), prostaglandin B2 (TPOAb -0.494), deoxycholic acid (TPOAb -0.470), traumatic acid (TPOAb -0.344, P=0.009), 13(S)-HOTrE (TgAb -0.276, P=0.040), chenodeoxycholic acid (FT4 -0.286, P=0.033; TgAb -0.264, P=0.049).
- In multivariable models, 7-methylxanthine, prostaglandin B2, prostaglandin F2alpha, deoxycholic acid and traumatic acid stayed inversely associated with TPOAb; 3-amino-pyrazolopyridine-diol stayed positive. For TgAb, 7-methylxanthine, prostaglandin F2alpha and chenodeoxycholic acid stayed inverse; caprylic acid and cadaverine stayed positive.

**Authors' stated limitations:** small sample powered only for large effects; some participants had TSH >2.5 mIU/L but subgrouping not possible; no multiple-testing correction; unmeasured confounders such as diet and stress; single region; no in vivo or in vitro functional validation, so findings are associative.

**Speculation (authors, not tested):** f_Clostridiales Incertae Sedis XI might contribute via molecular mimicry; reduced AA and ALA pathways might weaken gut antimicrobial defenses and favor Peptococcus; reduced ALA metabolism might limit maternal-fetal DHA supply.

## Relevance to Cureva

- Hypothesis only: inverse associations of fecal arachidonic acid, prostaglandins and secondary bile acids with TPOAb suggest a lipid-mediator and bile-acid axis worth testing; no intervention was studied.
- Lower birth weight in euthyroid antibody-positive women is a single-study signal in n=56 and does not establish microbial mediation.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- Taxa differences here (Peptococcus, Coprobacter, Rhodospirillaceae) do not overlap with taxa reported in other HT cohorts summarized by the authors (e.g., Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus decreased, Bacteroides fragilis increased; Clostridium sensu stricto 1, Ruminococcus torques and Fusicatenibacter increased); diversity also did not differ.
- Methods state Spearman correlation for microbe-metabolite analyses, while the Discussion says Pearson's correlation was applied.
- Pregnancy itself remodels the microbiome, limiting generalization to non-pregnant HT.

## Open Questions

- Are the metabolite differences microbial in origin, dietary, or host-derived?
- Do Peptococcus or Coprobacter shifts replicate in larger, multicentre and non-pregnant HT cohorts?
- Does the birth-weight difference persist in larger samples and is it mediated by thyroid autoimmunity, the microbiome, or neither?
