# Effect of selenium on thyroid autoimmunity and regulatory T cells in patients with Hashimoto's thyroiditis: A prospective randomized-controlled trial

## Key Findings

- **Selenium reduced thyroid antibody change scores vs untreated controls at 6 months**: ΔTPOAb median -28.4 (IQR -103.9 to 0) vs 0 (-18.1 to 20.5) IU/ml, p = 0.001; ΔTGAb -48.8 (-139.7 to -2.0) vs 18.3 (-23.5 to 77.4) IU/ml, p = 0.001. Absolute 6-month values did not differ (TPOAb 178.3 vs 211.3, p = 0.942; TGAb 388.0 vs 364.6, p = 0.891) (randomized-controlled-trial, open-label, no placebo, n = 90)
- **TSH change differed between groups**: ΔTSH -0.16 (-2.1 to 0.28) vs 0.48 (-0.15 to 1.47) mIU/L, p = 0.001; 6-month TSH 2.4 vs 3.24 mIU/L, p = 0.021, driven partly by a rise in controls. FT3 and FT4 did not differ (randomized-controlled-trial)
- **TSH benefit concentrated in subclinical HT**: ΔTSH -2.5 vs 1.91 mIU/L (p = 0.002, n = 29) vs 0.01 vs 0.38 in euthyroid HT (p = 0.079, n = 61); interaction p = 0.003; post hoc and small (randomized-controlled-trial, exploratory subgroup)
- **Selenium status markers rose**: serum Se 73.6 to 145.6 (3 months) to 187.2 ug/L (6 months), vs control 72.0 at 6 months (p < 0.001); GPx3 45.2 vs 24.2 ng/ml (p = 0.028); SePP1 17.2 vs 10.7 ng/ml (p = 0.007) (randomized-controlled-trial)
- **Treg subsets**: activated Treg (aTreg) % 13.19 plus or minus 3.5 vs 11.49 plus or minus 2.79 (p = 0.012); Helios/Treg 78.07 vs 72.55% (p = 0.003); Helios/aTreg 83.32 vs 79.97% (p = 0.002); total Treg 5.30 vs 4.89% (p = 0.214); PD-1 expression unchanged (randomized-controlled-trial, secondary outcomes)

## Methods

- **Design**: 6-month prospective, single-center, open-label, randomized, parallel controlled trial. Randomization by random-number generator program. No placebo and no blinding (the authors state "without a placebo control" as a limitation). Control group received no treatment. Registered ChiCTR2000032905; ethics approval 2017-SR-141.
- **Setting**: Endocrinology Department, First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University, China; October 2017 to February 2020.
- **Participants**: 155 recruited; 126 eligible and completed (90 HT patients, 36 healthy people used for baseline comparison only). HT patients not on levothyroxine; 61 euthyroid HT (EHT) and 29 subclinical HT (SHT).
  - Selenium group: n = 43, age 39.4 plus or minus 12.0 years, 39 F / 4 M, 27 EHT / 16 SHT.
  - Control group: n = 47, age 37.8 plus or minus 11.2 years, 41 F / 6 M, 34 EHT / 13 SHT.
  - Baseline groups did not differ significantly, though baseline TPOAb was numerically higher in the selenium group (237.3 vs 177.0 IU/ml, p = 0.107).
- **Intervention**: selenious yeast tablet (SYT) 200 ug/day for at least 6 months. Both groups received dietary iodine counseling to target urinary iodine 100 to 199 ug/L. No medication changes allowed.
- **Outcomes**: primary, change in TPOAb or TGAb. Secondary: FT3, FT4, TSH, serum Se (ICP-MS), GPx3 and SePP1 (ELISA), urinary iodine, Treg frequency and subsets (aTreg Foxp3hi CD45RO+, rTreg, sTreg) and Helios and PD-1 expression by flow cytometry. Measured at baseline, 3, and 6 months.
- **Statistics**: SPSS 25.0; sample size for 80% power, alpha 0.05 (details not given); t-test or Mann-Whitney U; interaction and stratified subgroup tests; Pearson or Spearman correlations; Bonferroni or Dunn's post hoc.

## Detailed Summary

### HT vs healthy controls at baseline

HT patients had higher median urinary iodine (246.6 vs 159.5 ug/L, p = 0.004), TPOAb (208.1 vs 13.2 IU/ml), TGAb, and TSH (2.92 vs 2.01 mIU/L) (all p < 0.001), and lower GPx3 (18.7 vs 22.1 ng/ml, p = 0.003). Serum Se (73.3 vs 76.9 ug/L, p = 0.493) and SePP1 (p = 0.085) did not differ significantly. Most participants were selenium deficient relative to an optimal level of about 125 ug/L.

### Selenium vs control over 6 months (Table 2)

- Serum Se: 3 months 145.6 vs 87.0 (p = 0.001); 6 months 187.2 vs 72.0 ug/L (p < 0.001).
- GPx3 at 6 months 45.2 vs 24.2 ng/ml (p = 0.028); SePP1 decreased in controls and was higher with selenium at 3 months (16.2 vs 10.8, p = 0.001) and 6 months (17.2 vs 10.7, p = 0.007).
- Urinary iodine fell in both groups to 150.5 vs 145.4 ug/L at 6 months (p = 0.793).
- TPOAb within the selenium group fell from 237.3 to 191.9 (3 months) and 178.3 IU/ml (6 months) (p < 0.01 vs baseline); controls 177.0 to 211.3 (not significant).
- TSH: selenium 3.16 to 2.4 mIU/L (not marked significant vs baseline); control 2.81 to 3.24 mIU/L (p < 0.01 vs baseline).
- FT3 and FT4 not different between groups at 3 or 6 months.
- No adverse effects reported (hair or nail loss, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, dizziness).

### Subgroups (Table 3; post hoc)

- Baseline Se below 80 ug/L (n = 49): ΔTPOAb -37.1 vs 2.9 (p = 0.001), ΔTGAb -43.3 vs 30.5 (p = 0.002), ΔTSH -0.63 vs 0.49 (p = 0.001). Se 80 ug/L or above (n = 41): not significant. Interaction p = 0.113, 0.687, 0.116 respectively, so no demonstrated effect modification by Se status.
- SHT: ΔTPOAb -86.5 vs 0.0 (p = 0.003); ΔTSH -2.5 vs 1.91 (p = 0.002). EHT: ΔTPOAb -6.8 vs -0.5 (p = 0.077); ΔTSH 0.01 vs 0.38 (p = 0.079). Interaction p = 0.054 (TPOAb), 0.853 (TGAb), 0.003 (TSH).

### Treg findings (Tables 4 and 5)

- Total Treg % rose within the selenium group (4.60 to 5.30, p < 0.01 vs baseline) but did not differ from control at 6 months (p = 0.214).
- aTreg, Helios/Treg, and Helios/aTreg were higher with selenium at 6 months (p = 0.012, 0.003, 0.002); Helios/aTreg already higher at 3 months (p = 0.002). rTreg, sTreg, and all PD-1 measures did not differ.
- Treg changes were similar in EHT and SHT (interaction p = 0.720, 0.668, 0.134).

### Correlations

At 6 months, Se correlated with GPx3 (r = 0.325, p = 0.002), SePP1 (r = 0.225, p = 0.033), and urinary iodine (r = 0.257, p = 0.014). The increase in Se correlated with changes in TPOAb (r = -0.278, p = 0.008), TGAb (r = -0.437, p = 0.003), TSH (r = -0.314, p < 0.001), aTreg (r = 0.275, p = 0.009), Helios/Treg (r = 0.286, p = 0.006), and Helios/aTreg (r = 0.277, p = 0.008). Baseline TPOAb and TGAb correlated inversely with Treg and aTreg percentages.

### Author-stated limitations

1. Open-label, single-center, small sample, without a placebo control.
2. Subgroup analysis by Se status not powered.
3. Mechanism of selenium action on Tregs not explained; functional studies needed.
4. Biochemical outcomes over a short period only; no data on symptoms, disease course, or levothyroxine need.

## Relevance to Cureva

- Intervention hypothesis: 200 ug/day selenium yeast for 6 months in levothyroxine-naive HT may reduce antibody trajectories and stabilize TSH, possibly more in subclinical HT; Treg (aTreg, Helios) shifts provide a candidate mechanism.
- Caution: no placebo or blinding, absolute antibody levels at 6 months were not different between groups, and the TSH difference partly reflects a rise in untreated controls.
- Monitoring signal: serum Se reached a median of 187.2 ug/L at 6 months, above the 122 ug/L threshold Huwiler 2024 cites as a level at which supplementation is not recommended.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- The paper describes the design as "randomized-controlled," and allocation was by random-number generator, but it was open-label with an untreated control; it was not placebo-controlled or blinded.
- Significant change-score differences coexist with non-significant absolute between-group differences at 6 months, and baseline TPOAb was numerically higher in the selenium group, raising possible regression-to-the-mean concerns.
- Huwiler 2024 coded this trial as no significant TPOAb or TGAb effect but decreased TSH, which differs from the authors' framing.
- Registration number ChiCTR2000032905 implies registration in 2020, while the trial ran from October 2017 (inference from the number format; registration date is not printed).
- Selenium supplementation pushed median serum Se well above the 80 to 120 ug/L optimal range the authors cite (U-shaped risk).

## Open Questions

- Would a placebo-controlled, blinded trial replicate the antibody and Treg effects?
- Do aTreg and Helios changes mediate antibody reduction, or are they parallel markers?
- Does selenium alter progression to overt hypothyroidism or levothyroxine need?
- Is there a gut microbiome component to selenium's immunomodulatory effects in HT (e.g. microbial selenium competition or SCFA-Treg interactions)?
