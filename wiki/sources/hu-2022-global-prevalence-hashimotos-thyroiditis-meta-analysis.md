# Global prevalence and epidemiological trends of Hashimoto's thyroiditis in adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis

## Key Findings

- **Global pooled adult prevalence 7.5% (95% CI 5.7 to 9.6%)**, random-effects, 48 studies, 22,680,155 participants; I2=100% (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Female predominance:** pooled odds ratio female vs male 3.86 (95% CI 2.33 to 6.39; random effects; 12 studies; I2=96%); text reports 17.5% prevalence in women vs 6.0% in men, "about four times" (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Decline over time:** studies grouped as before 2000 gave 9.0% (95% CI 5.7 to 12.9%; 21 studies; I2=99%) vs after 2000 6.5% (95% CI 4.3 to 9.0%; 27 studies; I2=100%) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Regional variation:** Africa 14.2% (2.5 to 32.9%), Oceania 11.0% (7.8 to 14.7%), Europe 8.0% (5.2 to 11.4%), South America 8.0% (2.0 to 17.5%), North America 7.8% (0.0 to 29.5%), Asia 5.8% (2.8 to 9.9%) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)
- **Income gradient:** lower-middle income 11.4% (2.5 to 25.2%), high income 8.4% (5.6 to 11.8%), upper-middle income 5.6% (3.9 to 7.4%) (systematic-review-meta-analysis)

## Methods

- **Design:** systematic review and meta-analysis, PRISMA-P, PROSPERO CRD42022339839. Databases: PubMed, Embase, MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, inception to June 2022, no language restriction.
- **Eligibility:** observational studies reporting adult HT prevalence; excluded n<100 or no full text/original data.
- **Included:** 48 studies (40 cross-sectional, 8 cohort), 37 population-based and 11 clinic-based, published 1966 to 2021, data from 1962 to 2021, 19 countries, 6 continents.
- **Case definition:** heterogeneous. 27 studies by serum autoantibodies alone; 9 autoantibodies plus ultrasound; 3 autoantibodies plus ultrasound plus FNA; 4 biopsy/pathology; 1 ultrasound alone; 4 not reported.
- **Quality:** modified Hoy checklist (0 to 9); all studies scored 6 or above; 42 rated low risk, 6 medium risk.
- **Analysis:** R 4.0.3 meta package; Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation (Shapiro-Wilk p=0.002); inverse variance; random effects when I2>50%. Egger's test and funnel plots; trim-and-fill; leave-one-out sensitivity analysis. Subgroups by income, region, sex, diagnostic method, time period, sample source.

## Detailed Summary

**Overall:** 7.5% (95% CI 5.7 to 9.6%), I2=100%, tau2=0.0171.

**Sex (Figure 5, 12 studies with sex-stratified data):** random-effects OR 3.86 (2.33 to 6.39); fixed-effect OR 3.51 (3.19 to 3.86); I2=96%, tau2=0.7429. Study ORs ranged from 1.14 (Bjøro 1984, Norway) to 18.91 (Konno 1993, Japan).

**Time period (Figure 4):** before 2000 9.0% (5.7 to 12.9%), after 2000 6.5% (4.3 to 9.0%). Grouping appears to follow the period of data collection rather than publication year (e.g., Pedersen 2003 sits in the before-2000 group). The authors also state the decline held in 20-year bins, with the lowest prevalence (6.7%) in 2000 to 2021, but those bin estimates are not shown with CIs.

**Region (Figure 7; k per region):** Africa 14.2% (2.5 to 32.9%; k=2; I2=95%), Oceania 11.0% (7.8 to 14.7%; k=2; I2=60%), Europe 8.0% (5.2 to 11.4%; k=20; I2=100%), South America 8.0% (2.0 to 17.5%; k=5), North America 7.8% (0.0 to 29.5%; k=3), Asia 5.8% (2.8 to 9.9%; k=16).

**Income (World Bank):** lower-middle 11.4% (2.5 to 25.2%), upper-middle 5.6% (3.9 to 7.4%), high 8.4% (5.6 to 11.8%).

**Diagnostic method:** serum autoantibodies 7.8% (5.4 to 10.5%); ultrasonography 13.2% (CI printed as 5.7 to 9.6%, which cannot contain the point estimate); pathology 12.5% (3.3 to 26.5%); antibodies plus colour Doppler ultrasound 10.4% (5.1 to 17.1%); antibodies plus ultrasound plus FNA 4.7% (0.0 to 21.0%). By sample type: serum 7.8% (5.8 to 10.1%), serum or tissue 7.6% (0.0 to 30.2%), thyroid tissue alone 14.1% (5.2 to 26.5%).

**Source:** clinic-based 8.6% vs population-based 7.5% in Results (7.2% in Discussion).

**Country extremes:** Tunisia 22.8% (20.3 to 25.4%), Denmark 18.8% (17.6 to 20.0%); South Korea 0.1%; Russia and Bosnia and Herzegovina 0.4%.

**Bias:** Egger's test indicated publication bias (P<0.05); trim-and-fill applied (adjusted estimate not reported in text). Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis showed stable results.

**Authors' limitations:** six decades of changing diagnostic criteria and assays; diagnostic criteria often unavailable; high heterogeneity unresolved by subgrouping; possible selection bias; few African studies; no follow-up for outcomes.

## Relevance to WikiBiome

- Baseline epidemiology for [[hashimotos-thyroiditis]] and [[thyroid-autoimmunity]]: about 1 in 13 adults in pooled data, with female odds roughly 4-fold.
- Because most included studies define HT by antibody positivity, these figures largely estimate thyroid autoantibody prevalence rather than clinically diagnosed disease.
- No microbiome, metal or diet exposures analyzed; authors speculate about micronutrients (vitamin D) and lifestyle as drivers of regional differences.

## Relevance to Cureva

- Useful for prior probability and population framing only; no intervention content.
- The sex OR supports considering sex-specific factors (see [[autoimmunity]]) when interpreting HT microbiome studies, which are predominantly female.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- **Decreasing trend contradicts most prior literature,** which reports rising autoimmune thyroid disease; authors attribute this to shifts in region mix and diagnostic methods over time.
- **Internal inconsistencies:** abstract lists 3 African studies and sums to 47 studies, whereas Results list 2 African and 2 Oceanian studies; population-based prevalence given as 7.5% (Results) and 7.2% (Discussion); ultrasound subgroup CI is incompatible with its point estimate; sex result described as prevalence ratio in text but pooled as an odds ratio in Figure 5.
- One Korean claims-based study (Kim 2021, 21,705,883 participants, 0.1%) contributes about 96% of all participants, so the headline n overstates the evidence base.
- The income pattern (high income above upper-middle income) is non-monotonic and based on wide CIs.

## Open Questions

- What is the prevalence of clinically confirmed HT (antibodies plus ultrasound or hypothyroidism) versus antibody positivity alone?
- Is the apparent decline real, or an artefact of assay and case-definition changes?
- Africa and Oceania estimates rest on 2 studies each; robust data are needed.
