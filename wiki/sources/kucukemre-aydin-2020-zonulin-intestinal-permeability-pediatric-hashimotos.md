# Children with Hashimoto's Thyroiditis Have Increased Intestinal Permeability: Results of a Pilot Study

## Key Findings

- **Serum zonulin was higher in children and adolescents with HT** than in age-, gender- and BMI-matched patients with congenital hypothyroidism (CH): 59.1±22.9 vs 43.3±32.9 ng/mL, p=0.035 (case-control)
- **In HT, zonulin correlated with weight (r=0.406, p=0.03), BMI (r=0.486, p=0.006) and levothyroxine dose (r=0.463, p=0.02)**; after adjusting for age, weight, TSH and fT4, only levothyroxine dose remained associated, with a borderline p-value (R2=0.36, p=0.05) (case-control)
- **Zonulin did not correlate with thyroid autoantibodies** in HT: anti-TPO r=-0.174, p=0.4; anti-TG r=0.295, p=0.1 (case-control)
- **In CH controls, zonulin tracked body size rather than thyroid status**: only weight remained associated in regression (R2=0.62, p<0.001), and there was no association with levothyroxine dose (p=0.4) (case-control)
- Authors interpret the HT-specific zonulin/levothyroxine association as possibly reflecting disease severity, but this is inference from a borderline result in a pilot study (case-control)

## Methods

- **Design**: Case-control pilot study (short communication)
- **Population**: 30 consecutive children and adolescents with HT and 30 patients with CH matched for age, gender and BMI; Istanbul, Turkey. HT diagnosed by positive anti-TPO, anti-TG and typical thyroiditis ultrasound pattern. Obese patients and those with acute or other chronic diseases excluded.
- **Groups**: HT age 12.6±2.7 y, F/M 25/5; CH age 11.3±3.4 y, F/M 22/8 (p=0.11 and 0.35). Both groups on levothyroxine (HT 1.2±0.84 vs CH 1.9±0.59 µg/kg/day, p<0.001).
- **Measurements**: Serum zonulin by ELISA (Elabscience); fT4, TSH, anti-TPO, anti-TG by ECLIA (Roche Cobas e602); thyroid ultrasound from records.
- **Analysis**: Independent t-test or chi-square; Pearson correlations; separate linear regression per group; SPSS 15.0; significance p≤0.05.

## Detailed Summary

- Zonulin: HT 59.1±22.9 vs CH 43.3±32.9 ng/mL, p=0.035.
- fT4 higher in CH (1.45±0.2 vs 1.28±0.2 ng/dL, p=0.004); TSH not different (HT 6.5±5.9 vs CH 7.7±6.3 mIU/L, p=0.37).
- Anti-TPO: HT 268.2±201 vs CH 11.4±3 IU/mL (p<0.001). Anti-TG: HT 641.4±1076 vs CH 12.3±3.1 IU/mL (p<0.001).
- Ultrasound in HT: heterogeneous parenchyma in 17, heterogeneous plus pseudo-nodular in 13. In CH: agenesis 15, heterogeneous 3, normal 12.
- HT correlations with zonulin: weight r=0.406 (p=0.03), weight SDS r=0.377 (p=0.04), BMI r=0.486 (p=0.006), BMI-SDS r=0.419 (p=0.02), levothyroxine dose r=0.463 (p=0.02).
- CH correlations with zonulin: age r=0.475 (p=0.008), weight r=0.707 (p<0.001), weight SDS r=0.532 (p=0.002), BMI r=0.872 (p<0.001), BMI-SDS r=0.681 (p<0.001), levothyroxine dose r=0.485 (p=0.007).
- Regression (adjusted for age, weight, TSH, fT4): HT, zonulin associated only with levothyroxine dose (R2=0.36, p=0.05); CH, only weight (R2=0.62, p<0.001).
- Intestinal permeability itself was not measured directly (no lactulose/mannitol test); "increased intestinal permeability" is inferred from serum zonulin.

## Relevance to Cureva

- Weak hypothesis support only: a barrier-directed approach (e.g. zonulin pathway) in HT is not tested here. The authors mention the zonulin antagonist AT-1001 (larazotide) based on type 1 diabetes and celiac literature, not HT data.
- Zonulin correlates strongly with weight and BMI in both groups, so any use as a monitoring marker must adjust for body size.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- Control group was CH (also hypothyroid and on levothyroxine), not healthy children; the authors list the absence of a healthy control group as a limitation.
- Commercial zonulin ELISA kits have been questioned in the wider literature for specificity; this paper does not address assay validity.
- The adjusted HT association with levothyroxine dose is borderline (p=0.05) and the severity interpretation is speculative.

## Open Questions

- Does serum zonulin elevation precede HT onset, as reported for type 1 diabetes?
- Would direct permeability tests (lactulose/mannitol) confirm the zonulin signal in pediatric HT?
- Is zonulin related to gut microbiome composition in these children (not measured)?
