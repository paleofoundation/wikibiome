## Identity and morphology evidence

- `trichosporon-asahii`: current species `NCBITaxon:82508`; type material includes CBS 2479, IGC 3469, JCM 2466, NCYC 2677, and UAMH 7654. NCBI confirms the current species and type material. Published microscopy describes hyaline septate hyphae, barrel-like arthroconidia, and blastoconidia. A restrained fragmented-hypha plate shows these structures without colony or disease context.
- `turicibacter`: current genus `NCBITaxon:191303`; type species *Turicibacter sanguinis*, type strain MOL361 / DSM 14220 / CCUG 48620. NCBI Taxonomy, LPSN, and the original description confirm a strictly anaerobic rod-shaped type species. The plate uses separated vegetative rods and makes no strain-wide sporulation claim.
- `tyzzerella`: `NCBITaxon:1506577` remains an NCBI current effective-name record, but LPSN states that “*Tyzzerella*” was not validly published, has no standing, is a synonym, and has *Faecalimonas* as the correct genus. Its proposed type “*Tyzzerella nexilis*” likewise lacks standing. No prompt or image is authorized pending canonical reconciliation.
- `ureaplasma`: current genus `NCBITaxon:2129`; type species *Ureaplasma urealyticum*. NCBI and LPSN confirm the genus and type. Authoritative reviews describe very small pleomorphic cells lacking a peptidoglycan wall and bounded by a plasma membrane. The plate shows softly irregular spherical-to-ovoid bodies without implying a rigid cell wall or diagnostic form.
- `ureaplasma-urealyticum`: current species `NCBITaxon:2130`; type strain T960 / ATCC 27618 / CIP 103755 / NCTC 10177. NCBI and LPSN confirm the current emended species and type strain. The species plate uses a different restrained arrangement of small pleomorphic membrane-bounded bodies and does not claim visual separability from other *Ureaplasma* species.
- `veillonella`: current genus `NCBITaxon:29465`; type species *Veillonella parvula*. NCBI and LPSN confirm the current genus and type. Type-strain genomic description records small nonmotile, non-spore-forming anaerobic cocci about 0.3–0.5 micrometres in diameter occurring in pairs or short chains. The plate therefore shows only countable pairs and short chains.
