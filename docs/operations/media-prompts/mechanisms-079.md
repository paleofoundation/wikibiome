## Route audits

### `lipid-peroxidation`

MeSH D015227 identifies lipid peroxidation, but the route contains only selected metal drivers, three generalized microbiome mechanisms, and cross-links. It calls heavy metals potent initiators as a class, glutathione the primary endogenous defense, commensal glutathione production a host antioxidant source, butyrate protective through mitochondria, and microbial siderophores regulators of the host Fenton substrate without defining organism, tissue, membrane, lipid, exposure, concentration, initiation pathway, marker, or assay. Separate enzymatic and nonenzymatic oxidation, lipid class and compartment, initiation/propagation/termination, biomarkers and measurement limits, and human versus model evidence before selecting a reaction or membrane representation.

Authorities: https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/record/ui?ui=D015227 ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/lipid-peroxidation

### `lipopolysaccharide`

MeSH D008070 defines a heterogeneous class with lipid A, core, and O-specific chains and recommends coordination with the source bacterium. The page acknowledges structural variation but then treats gut LPS as one disease-driving exposure, calls barrier dysfunction a prerequisite for translocation, labels Proteobacteria the primary source of pathogenic LPS, and converts adipose, endothelial, plaque, liver, metal, and nutritional-immunity associations into a unified causal chain. TLR recognition depends on lipid A chemistry, aggregation, binding proteins, dose, compartment, host species, and assay; not all Gram-negative outer-membrane glycolipids are equivalent. Specify organism/strain, chemotype, molecular species, host, compartment, dose, purity, receptor system, and evidence tier before drawing a molecular or tissue-signaling plate.

Authorities: https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/record/ui?ui=D008070 ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/lipopolysaccharide

### `maternal-immune-activation`

Maternal immune activation is a research hypothesis and family of experimental models, not one exposure or diagnosis. The article says a cytokine storm reaches the fetus, IL-6 crosses the placenta and disrupts the fetal brain, microglia remain persistently activated, a fixed dysbiosis persists into adulthood, metals synergize, and *Bacteroides fragilis* treatment provides direct evidence of mediation. Human evidence is observational and cannot control timing, chronicity, severity, co-exposures, or confounding; mechanistic causal control comes mainly from heterogeneous animal models using agents such as poly(I:C) or LPS. Separate human epidemiology from each animal model, exposure, gestational timing, maternal/placental/fetal compartment, species, sex, outcome, and intervention before considering a maternal-fetal illustration.

Authorities: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8754471/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9021321/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37123361/ ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/maternal-immune-activation

### `matrix-metalloproteases`

MeSH D020782 defines a family of zinc-dependent human matrix metalloendopeptidases and advises using specific enzymes where possible. The route calls MMPs critical for every metastatic step, explains an observational dietary-zinc/endometriosis association through increased MMP-2/-9 activity, conflates human MMPs with unrelated bacterial and fungal metalloproteases, and asserts direct substitution by cadmium or nickel, ROS activation, TIMP suppression, and zinc-excess tissue invasion. Family members differ in domains, activation, substrates, location, inhibitors, and disease context; zinc sufficiency does not imply activity tracks dietary intake. Separate human MMPs from microbial protease families and bind each claim to a specific enzyme, structure, activation state, tissue, substrate, exposure, and study design before choosing a family or enzyme plate.

Authorities: https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/record/ui?ui=D020782 ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/matrix-metalloproteases

### `mediterranean-diet`

The Mediterranean diet is a variable dietary pattern, not one dosage or fixed food list. The page is duplicated within the source corpus and presents PCOS associations as direct effects, specifies universal servings and wine allowances, estimates nickel sensitivity prevalence, requires screening before prescribing the pattern for several conditions, labels it potentially harmful, and offers low-nickel substitutions including refined grains. The cited observational nickel/endometriosis work cannot establish universal screening or contraindication policy, and food nickel varies by source and preparation. Reconcile the duplicate records; define the dietary index and population; separate observational, randomized, and guideline evidence; remove unsupported screening, contraindication, and treatment prescriptions; and distinguish allergy diagnosis from population prevalence before a food plate is considered.

Authorities: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/mesh/?term=Mediterranean+Diet ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/mediterranean-diet

### `mendelian-randomization`

Mendelian randomization is an instrumental-variable method whose causal interpretation depends on relevance, independence, and exclusion-restriction assumptions. The page calls microbiome MR the causal backbone of WikiBiome, labels individual taxa causal drivers or protectors, treats reverse MR as proof of consequence, and says only causally implicated taxa are rational intervention targets. MiBioGen used heterogeneous 16S data from 18,340 people; few genera were consistently detected and most loci were suggestive. Microbiome instruments can be weak, taxonomically coarse, ancestry-dependent, pleiotropic, and biologically nonspecific, and sensitivity tests cannot prove all assumptions. Reframe the route around estimands, assumptions, instrument selection, harmonization, multiplicity, replication, sensitivity analyses, ancestry and taxonomic limits, and cautious interpretation before drawing a causal graph.

Authorities: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7217228/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6041728/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33462485/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11389083/ ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/mendelian-randomization
