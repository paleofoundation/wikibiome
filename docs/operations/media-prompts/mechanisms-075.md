## Route audits

### `hyperparathyroidism`

The article acknowledges primary disease but then centers an asserted heavy-metal → renal hydroxylation → vitamin-D deficiency → secondary hyperparathyroidism pathway derived largely from one rheumatic-disease association. It treats extreme correlations as causal, says metals explain rheumatoid-arthritis vitamin-D deficiency and inflammation, claims PTH raises intestinal toxic-metal absorption and mobilizes bone cadmium, and proposes chelation. KDIGO instead describes CKD secondary hyperparathyroidism as complex and evaluates phosphate, calcium, intake, vitamin-D deficiency, kidney function, and FGF23; a single PTH value does not establish the page's causal chain. Correct the disease taxonomy, distinguish primary/secondary/tertiary disease, verify the Haddad source and units/design, remove causal and chelation claims, and use clinical guidance before reconsidering normal parathyroid-context media.

Authorities: https://id.nlm.nih.gov/mesh/D006961 ; https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/record/ui?ui=D049950 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6340919/ ; https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/KDIGO-2025-CKD-MBD-Controversies-Conference-Report_Final.pdf

### `hypoxia`

The page defines hypoxia as tissue oxygen below 5% against 21% air, although tissue oxygen is organ-, compartment-, method-, and context-dependent and the healthy intestine normally has a steep physiologic oxygen gradient. It describes *Faecalibacterium* as an obligate aerobe, predicts simple crashes/thriving of broad taxa, links permeability to oxygen diffusion without adequate sourcing, calls H. pylori urease an energy source, and universalizes iron, nickel, sulfide, immune, biofilm, estrogen, and cancer consequences. It also has broken empty cross-reference labels. Replace the universal threshold with measured-context terminology, distinguish physiologic from inflammatory/tumour hypoxia, correct organism physiology and urease function, source each ecological claim, and repair the broken links before neutral tissue-oxygen media is reconsidered.

Authorities: https://id.nlm.nih.gov/mesh/D000860 ; https://journals.physiology.org/doi/10.1152/ajpcell.00191.2015 ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK2417/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7934918/

### `hypoxic-signaling`

The page title claims the HIF-1α pathway but opens as a nickel-carcinogenesis mechanism and cites numerous unrelated secondary sources. It says nickel uniquely distinguishes arsenic/chromium without an adequate comparative basis, conflates HIF-1 with its alpha subunit, treats proposed metal substitution and ascorbate depletion as one settled pathway, and contains a chemically incoherent sentence in which “the oxygen of the Ni(II) hydroxyl group at the proline C4 position” supposedly explains VHL recognition—the recognition chemistry concerns hydroxylated proline, not a nickel hydroxyl group. Bound HIF-1/HIF-1α identity, separate normoxic regulation from metal-induced hypoxia-like signaling, correct the prolyl-hydroxylation chemistry, and grade cell/model evidence before a pathway plate is reconsidered.

Authorities: https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/record/ui?ui=D051793 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1257550/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15866766/

### `il-10`

The article calls IL-10 the primary anti-inflammatory cytokine and IL-10 dysfunction the substrate of chronic inflammatory disease, while the knockout-colitis phenotype depends strongly on microbial and housing context. It converts isolated disease measurements and a Mendelian-randomization association into a proposed therapeutic IL-10:IL-6 target, and labels peritoneal-fluid and plasma findings as general disease mechanisms. Define human IL-10 separately from receptor signaling and model systems; state cell sources and effects contextually; bound the knockout model to microbiota and housing; verify each cohort, compartment, assay, and direction; and remove untested therapeutic targets before a protein-context image is considered.

Authorities: https://id.nlm.nih.gov/mesh/D016753 ; https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P22301/entry ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4741283/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6352895/

### `il-17`

The article alternates between the IL-17 family, IL-17A, and IL-17F, calls IL-17 a Th17 signature despite other cellular sources, and moves from murine host-specific segmented-filamentous-bacteria experiments to a generic human microbiome rule. It presents a single low/high axis for barrier defense versus autoimmune destruction and names clinical targets without noting disease-specific failures or worsening seen with IL-17 blockade in Crohn disease. Define the family and exact ligand(s), distinguish producers and receptors, identify murine versus human evidence, preserve protective and pathogenic tissue context, and align therapy claims with indication-specific evidence before media is reconsidered.

Authorities: https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/record/ui?ui=D020381 ; https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q16552/entry ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6050222/ ; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41385-020-00335-w ; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41385-018-0050-3

### `il-1beta`

The page calls IL-1β the primary product of NLRP3 and the most tightly regulated inflammatory cytokine, although multiple inflammasome sensors and non-canonical or extracellular proteases can process IL-1β in context. It says metals uniquely provide both priming and activation signals, generalizes distinct elements and chemical forms, and turns isolated cell/cohort results into broad ASD, schizophrenia, and IBD-to-erectile-dysfunction mechanisms. Define human IL-1β and its precursor, distinguish NLRP3 from other processing routes, bound priming/activation by material, form, dose, cell type, and assay, and remove unsupported disease propagation language before protein/inflammasome media is reconsidered.

Authorities: https://id.nlm.nih.gov/mesh/D053583 ; https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P01584/entry ; https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/record/ui?ui=D007375 ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/il-1beta
