## Route audits

### `microglia`

Microglia are resident central-nervous-system macrophages with dynamic, multidimensional states shaped by development, region, age, sex, disease, and experimental context. The page acknowledges but still organizes biology around “resting,” M1, and M2 states, assigns fixed damaging versus reparative cytokine programs, and states that metals and gut signals make microglia the cellular mediator through which the gut–brain axis produces neurodegeneration. Current nomenclature guidance rejects resting/activated and M1/M2 dichotomies as inconsistent with observed state diversity. It also generalizes exposure-specific in-vitro and animal findings into persistent activation and disease mechanisms. Rebuild around species, age, brain region, disease/model, cell-identification markers, transcriptomic/proteomic state, exposure dose/route/time, comparator, and measured function before selecting a cell-state image.

Authorities: https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/record/ui?ui=D017628 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=36327895 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41991797/ ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/microglia

### `mis-metallation`

Protein mismetalation is context-dependent competition for metal-binding sites under defined intracellular availabilities; it is not a universal explanation for every toxic-metal, transport, endocrine, thyroid, microbial-competition, or nutritional-immunity effect. The route includes direct metal substitution, enzyme inhibition, ligand mimicry, membrane transport, metallothionein binding, siderophore competition, host metal sequestration/flooding, epidemiological metal patterns, and therapeutic supplementation. It presents many exact concentrations, disease claims, and 2025–2026 models as established general rules. Current reviews emphasize that biological metalation depends on competing sites, buffered pools, compartment, organism, protein, and experimental evidence; some metalloenzymes can accept multiple metals. Split by named protein/accession, organism/compartment, cognate and competing chemical species, site/stoichiometry, metal availability, assay, phenotype, and in-vivo evidence before generating a molecular mechanism.

Authorities: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34763208/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38960447/ ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/mis-metallation

### `mitochondrial-dysfunction`

“Mitochondrial dysfunction” can denote changes in respiration, membrane potential, substrate use, redox state, dynamics, quality control, calcium handling, signaling, or cell survival, and no single blood biomarker reveals all underlying dysfunction. The page treats mitochondria as primary targets of all heavy metals, assigns element-specific molecular lesions, connects those lesions to ferroptosis, microbiome metabolites, several neurological and metabolic diseases, and implies temporal precedence or causality. It does not consistently specify metal species, dose, tissue, model, mitochondrial preparation, assay, normalization, or whether dysfunction is cause, consequence, or correlate. Rebuild by tissue/cell/model, perturbation, dose/time, respiratory substrate, ETC assay, membrane potential, ROS method, morphology/mitophagy measure, comparator, and causal intervention before an organelle image is considered.

Authorities: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34740866/ ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/mitochondrial-dysfunction

### `molecular-mimicry`

Molecular mimicry requires a defined foreign epitope, self epitope, sequence or structural similarity, cross-reactive antibody or T-cell evidence, exposure association, and ideally reproducible pathogenicity. The page correctly cites Campylobacter lipooligosaccharide and Guillain–Barré syndrome but then treats proposed microbiome links to type 1 diabetes, Graves disease, Hashimoto thyroiditis, and multiple sclerosis as equivalent established examples. It further proposes that metal-driven dysbiosis increases mimicry risk without direct epitope evidence. Reviews describe substantial indirect evidence and plausible contribution but limited definitive human causation, with alternative mechanisms including bystander activation, epitope spreading, persistence, and dual T-cell receptors. Rebuild each example around exact microbial/self antigens, epitope coordinates/structure, HLA, cross-reactivity assay, patient cohort, epidemiology, animal transfer/model, and alternative mechanisms before media generation.

Authorities: https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/record/ui?ui=D008969 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22095454/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35572569/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36136068/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38415936/ ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/molecular-mimicry

### `mycobiome`

The human mycobiome varies by body site and is methodologically difficult because fungi are low abundance, reference databases are incomplete, and studies face culture, extraction, primer, sequencing, contamination, transient dietary/environmental signal, and taxonomic-resolution limits. The page gives a universal gut abundance range, calls selected genera commensal or pathogenic, generalizes enrichment across cardiometabolic disease and cancer, claims fungi reach the colon within 30 minutes, and presents antifungal, immune, biofilm, and metal interactions as integrated mechanisms. Recent reviews describe heterogeneous methods, inconsistent taxa, and major translation barriers. Rebuild by body site, specimen, biomass controls, culture versus sequencing method, primers/database, absolute burden, viability/residency evidence, species/strain, confounders, cohort replication, and functional validation before depicting a community.

Authorities: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39189314/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40278100/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40990048/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41369685/ ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/mycobiome

### `n-glycosylation`

N-glycosylation differs across eukaryotes, bacteria, and fungi in glycan precursors, sequons, enzymes, compartments, substrates, and biological functions; host glycome associations are not equivalent to microbial protein N-glycosylation. The page combines host protein folding and immunity, a study correlating vaginal taxa with serum/urine IgG N-glycans, fungal immune masking, biofilms, and a claim that infection metallomics alters metal-dependent glycosyltransferases/glycosidases. The cited endometriosis study reports correlations and explicitly calls for functional work; bacterial glycosylation reviews distinguish N-, O-, and atypical linkages. Select organism, compartment, substrate protein/site, glycan composition/structure, enzyme/pathway identifiers, analytical method, and demonstrated phenotype; remove or substantiate the metal-dependence claim before drawing a pathway.

Authorities: https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/record/ui?ui=D006026 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25936979/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26433695/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37741057/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39455640/ ; local:data/content.generated.json#/pages/n-glycosylation
